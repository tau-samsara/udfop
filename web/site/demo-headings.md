Headings split a page into sections. The page title is the first heading, so a page starts its own sections at the second level (`##`). The first three levels (`##`, `###` and `####` here) build the **Contents** list on a wide screen, and on a phone each section folds shut until it is tapped.

## A section

Text under a second-level heading. Point at the heading to see the small `#` in the left margin: it copies the address of this exact section.

### A smaller section

Third-level headings are listed under their section in the Contents list, behind a small arrow.

#### A heading of the fourth level

Fourth-level headings are shown as headings, but they are not listed in the Contents or linked from it.

## Linking to a section

A link can name a section of this page, such as [[#A smaller section]], or a section of another page, such as [[site:Demo: Links#Other links on this site]]. Capitals and punctuation do not matter.

## How the Contents list follows you

Scroll down this page: the entry of the section you are reading is marked in the Contents list. The last sections are short, so the page leaves extra room at the end and the last heading can still reach the top of the window.

## A last section

This is the last section. A link to it still lands with its heading at the top of the window.
