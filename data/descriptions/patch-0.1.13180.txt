Abyss Dungeons ||| An Abyss Dungeon is the hardest dungeon in the game, online only. One at a time rises beside a busy city in the region with the most players online, with a column of brass-gold light over it, and the first player to reach its door finds it and everyone online hears where, after which its ring, compass mark, Timers row and notice-board note stay until it is gone. It has three foes at every spot, each four times as tough and hitting two and a half times as hard, six elite foes, half again the loot and no campfires. At its deepest point stands the Rift into the Shattered Hour, where the Orrery of Endings, the Unmoored Steps and the Brass Remnant are met, and the Remnant's fight, spoils, collapse and one-life rule are shared by everyone in the Hour at once. Each Abyss Dungeon keeps one of six Endings and two of nine omens, and fighters earn Hours broken toward the Hourbreaker title and The Turning Hour aura. Crew and sworn companions wait outside the Rift, and leaving the Hour for any reason puts the player on the ground at the dungeon's door. A shared Shattered Hour for players outside a party holds 40 players, where it held 8.
Accounts ||| Accounts hold a player's login and their cloud saves, served by an account Worker that issues tokens and checks passwords. The service was built in 0.1.3607 and 0.1.3690, then repaired after an outage caused by an overly costly password-hashing setting on Cloudflare, and later given a clock fix. The account server has since been protected from overload: after it fell over again in 0.1.6625 games now wait longer after each failed ask instead of retrying at once, and in 0.1.6640 signing in, joining a room and loading a town's house layouts were made far cheaper for it. The letterbox, the count over a town's Notice Boards and the play time now share one request to the account service instead of three, cutting its database work by about a third per player. A username can no longer be an email address: a player who signed up with one still signs in with it but other players see a generated name. After a server update players no longer all reconnect at once, each game waiting a moment (longer the more players are online), sign-in token requests and the account, realm, heartbeat and profession reads are answered cheaply from read copies of the database, the letterbox, Notice Board count and play time share one request, and when many players sign in at once they wait in a short line instead of getting a "server" error. Most menus that talk to the server (the Guild tab, the market, the Seat tab, letters, the account card and signing in) load much faster, since the account server now runs next to its database instead of across the Pacific, and joining the world, the chat's player list and the friends list come up sooner after signing in. When the servers are overloaded the game holds off on loading homes, yards, seats, traders, guild invitations, Marks and the save list and asks again later, waiting longer the longer the overload lasts, while actions such as buying, crafting, saving and claiming still go through, and the servers also turn those requests away for a moment to recover. Town homes, rooms and decorations, gathering nodes, traders, Marks, guild invites, lineages, unions, cloud saves, the Arena board and the town seats list now load from read copies of the database instead of the one main database. Logging in, saving and the town, seat and notice-board panels stay responsive at peak hours, as several of the busiest requests ask the account database far fewer times, and when the service is overloaded the game backs off its background checks (mail, the notice board, the day's motherlodes) instead of asking again at once. When the account server is busy it sends town and display data from its extra copies, which can be some seconds old but do not fail, so sign in, saves and town data fail less with many players online; the game asks the account service less often when walking through the world, uses fewer server requests when signing in to a new area and waits longer before asking a busy server again. A stop of up to a minute that slowed sign in, saves and homes was fixed.
Death Penalty ||| The death penalty is the gold lost when dying. It was a quarter of the gold in the purse, and since 0.1.6068 it is a tenth online. Three coins or fewer lose nothing, bank gold and letters of credit are safe, the death screen says exactly how much before the player rises, and a resurrection by a party member costs nothing. Offline, when Project Legacy raises the player after a death they now also lose a tenth of the gold they carry. Online a dead player can rise after 15 seconds with Enter, or rises after one minute, giving a party member time to Resurrect them, in and out of the PvP zone (the zone wait was two minutes). A player killed in the PvP zone loses no gold or letters of credit: the killer gets one uninsured worn item, or the gold of one policy if all are insured, or, with neither, one tenth of the coin and letters of credit, coin first, as coin or one letter of credit if too heavy.
Enhanced AI ||| Enhanced AI changes how foes fight. A foe waiting its turn turns and fights when approached instead of backing away, the step back after a blow is under half a second, and hurt foes back off at most once every 10 seconds. An archer waiting its turn stands still facing the player, retreats once for up to 2 seconds when closed on, then fights hand to hand and goes back to shooting once the player is about 6 m away. It is on by default offline as well as online, and can be switched back to the classic enemy AI in Settings > Combat > Enemies, though pressing All off in the features list keeps it off. A stuck foe stops casting about 550 rays every step.
Item Insurance ||| A magic or crafted item can be insured outside the open PvP zone with Insure on its card in the pack, for a fifth of its value, starting after two minutes. If a player kills the owner the killer cannot take an insured item, the insurance ends only when it saves the item, and a death never takes the cargo of the player's cart or the companions' packs. If every worn item of a killed player is insured, the killer gets the gold of one policy (a letter of credit if too heavy) and the victim keeps the item while the policy ends; the insurance of a villain killed by a player keeps no item.
Launch Speed ||| The first launch after an update no longer freezes for half a minute while the game prepares its graphics, as the work runs in the background while the world loads, and the first seconds of play no longer stop while effects such as weapon glows and foe warnings get ready. On a slower or busy computer the volumetric clouds no longer stop the first launch, the sky showing simpler clouds until the full ones are ready, and the pictures of the character's body load in the background. The game keeps some results of the first launch (town ground, ship models and pictures, shipyard and lighthouse sites, and the map of Tamriel), using about 9 MB of storage, which makes the first launch a little slower and later launches' pauses in Daggerfall shorter. After logging in, the game prepares effect shaders one at a time instead of all at once, which removed a freeze of 10 to 15 seconds on some computers, though other players' auras can appear some seconds after the players. After an update the water and rain graphics prepare in the background, so the game does not stop for some seconds on entering the world, and the desktop app removes its old graphics cache after each update to prevent a slow start.
Lifts ||| A lift stands its bucket again without dropping the broad filing.
Living World ||| The living world makes every town person a permanent resident with a name, face, home, trade, daily schedule, watch shifts, tavern/shop/temple routines, guild memberships, evening gatherings, lodgings, and travel between towns. Player actions (kindness, help, theft, crime) affect social standing and permanent household hostility. It is on by default under Features (World), walled city streets are populated, and talking to a Living World resident displays how they regard you under their name. A large city has more people in its houses and streets than a small town (Daggerfall has 591, about 90 near its main square at ten in the morning), while villages and hamlets do not change. A cutpurse can take a purse from a person in a crowd and run, with a nearby guard running after, in the evening two people can play dice or become friendly, a priest can speak the god's name at a temple door and a courier can call out the news. The game does less work at once preparing the news travellers bring, and prepares a town's daily events more quickly.
Open PvP Zone ||| Cyrodiil is the open PvP zone, exactly the province with its lakes and rivers, and the red line, fog and edge of the zone map follow its border; the map shows the zone offline too, with its fog, red line and rings, and its city and town names. The streets of each city, town and village are a safe zone where no player can hit or cast at the player, and a message tells when the player enters or leaves; the open land, roads, dungeons, halls, farms, manors, inns and temples are not safe. A monster's kill returns the player to a town of Cyrodiil in the same ring and a player's kill to the nearest town outside the zone. For a minute after a hit between players items cannot be put into storage, a second kill by the same player within 30 minutes takes nothing, and for 60 seconds after entering or coming back to life no player can hurt the player, ended by attacking a player or taking items from remains. The Wrothgarian Mountains are a usual region again, and the Lake Rumare and Niben inside the province are part of the zone. Since 0.1.13148 a death in a PvP zone returns the player to a town and gives no safe passage on return, safe passage coming only from walking across the zone's edge, not by teleporting or leaving a town or dungeon. Killing a player gives one random worn item that is not insured, and the victim does not drop items in the bag. The streets are not safe for a villain: a villain can be attacked from the streets, cannot attack back from them, and the town guards do not come when a villain is attacked there.
Player Names ||| The names of other players show only when looked at near them and fade in and out slowly with their skull and bars, in all areas; the names of the party always show, as does that of a player hit or hit by the player in the last minute.
PvP Combat ||| In a fight with a player armour decreases the damage of hits, better materials more, each kind differently against blades, blunt weapons and hands, and worn armour protects less; resistances decrease the damage of spells, with a Resist spell cutting its element and race, Willpower and gear resistances also counting, at most 85% stopped even if immune. Each class has a small advantage over one other (archers over mages, mages over warriors, warriors over archers), arrows do more damage to players and spells a little less, more so from level 40, and a Shield spell stops 25% of weapon and 35% of arrow damage. Spells cast on oneself last at most 1 minute, only one potion of each kind (health, magicka, other) can be drunk in 15 seconds and gives only its first effect, and each plate piece raises spell magicka cost by 6% and slows casts 3% (a chain piece half, leather none; full plate 42% and 21%). A spell at a player uses only its first effect, can critically hit by Critical Strike and Luck, and only Damage effects and heals work on players, not Drain or poison; weapon skill and gear damage bonus raise hits on players.
PvP Flag ||| The PvP zones are Cyrodiil and the open sea, where Caps Lock or the button at the top right puts the PvP flag on or off from level 30. Without the flag attacks and spells do not hit players, so monsters can be fought safely near them; with it the player can attack all players, cannot remove it for a minute after a hit in a PvP fight, and without it can use a Portal Stone anywhere in the zone. Below level 30 players cannot attack or be attacked, and the game asks before going from level 29 to 30, where Not yet keeps level 29. A skull shows above a flagged player's name, white, gold for a minute after a PvP hit, red for a villain, also on the button and the Overworld view, and a flagged player has a red health bar below it with a thin blue line for a Shield spell, a hidden player showing a faint body, the skull and the bar but no name. With the flag up Levitate stops and cannot be cast, buffs stop and cannot be cast on oneself except Shield, and the player cannot rest, wait, ride a horse or a cart, while run speed and jump are limited to the best a character can have without magic and Paralyze does not hit a player.
PvP Health ||| In the open zone in Cyrodiil, in a duel and in a siege, a hit from a player removes the player's own health, shown on the health bar, as a part of health, so a strong and a weak character lose the same part from the same hit. For 30 seconds after a player hit, and throughout a duel or siege, the player cannot heal, and health from a potion or spell in that time does not stay; health a player removes does not return by itself after 30 seconds. A lost duel ends with 1 health and does not heal, and in a siege the player returns with full health at their wave. Since 0.1.13148 a fight with a player uses the real maximum health, up to 3,000, where one hit or spell removes at most 34% of it, so at least three hits kill, and the old limit on damage in five seconds is gone, the weapon speed and spell limit setting how fast damage comes. After a player's hit, for 30 seconds all heals together give at most 25% of maximum health in 15 seconds (up to 35% with a high Restoration skill), and duels now allow healing under the same limit.
PvP Spell Bar ||| With the PvP flag on in a PvP zone keys 3 to 0 show a PvP spell bar and keys 1 and 2 are for potions, and only spells on the bar can be cast, each costing a part of maximum magicka. The bar has Damage (an area spell with a chosen element), Heal, Shield, Resist, Reflect, Absorb, Fortify, Cure, Jump, Speed and Resurrect; a slot is changed by releasing the mouse and clicking it, choosing spell, element or attribute and icon. A Heal or buff goes to the party member at the crosshair up to 15 m away, or to the player if none, and Resurrect costs 25% of maximum magicka and brings back a fallen party member whose body is near, not a player outside the party.
Regional Names ||| Each people of Tamriel has its own names. A Nord can have a clan name such as Battle-Born or the father's name with -sen, -son or -sdottir; an Orc has gro- or gra- before a parent's name; an Argonian can be named like Hides-In-Mud; a Khajiit can have a title before the name, such as Ri'saad; a Redguard can have at- and a parent's name, or al- and a city's name; and a Dark Elf of an old Great House can have the house first. Each region has its own families whose names are heard again there, on the Iliac Bay Daggerfall's names are also heard, and the people of a quest already started keep their names while others in a town can have a new name.
Render Loop ||| The render loop had its judder removed and stopped feeding the garbage collector. One eye array is used for the world frame's passes.
Town Watch ||| The town watch keeps the town day and night in three eight-hour shifts plus a day off. Patrols walk in pairs around their own district, in larger towns a watchman stands at each gate, off duty he wears his own clothes as a townsperson, and larger towns keep a larger watch. In the 14 cities whose palace stands in its own walled grounds the watch now lives where its door opens onto the streets, so it walks its patrols instead of staying at the palace. A watchman on duty greets the player by their standing with the region's law, a first minor offence in a region draws a Hold! warning and not an arrest (the next brings one), and the watch shouts "Halt!" from one watchman at most once every 15 seconds. A monster that gets into town can attack townspeople, one blow killing one, and the watch defends them as it does the player. Paying a fine no longer counts as killing the watchman who walked away. All of this is in the living world, and Classic play keeps Daggerfall's own watch and townspeople. The watch posted at a city's gates stops strangers coming in and asks their business, and strangers answer civilly, curtly or with open hostility, sellswords and sailors rougher; patrols stop strangers on their rounds, every stranger stopped at a gate is questioned, and a gate stop no longer keeps a visitor from leaving. The watch breaks up a quarrel even at a change of shift, and only one of a patrol pair calls the hour. When a monster attacks in town the guards still come to help but walk off after the fight instead of vanishing, leaving the world only once far away or behind the player, and if another monster turns up while they walk off the same guards turn back to the fight, a guard attacked on his way out defending himself before carrying on. Paying the watch's fine on the spot raises standing in that region by 5, up to the point where the watch stops recognising the player, where a paid fine only bought a day without stops before. Guards called into a building walk round through the doorway to reach the player, even between deep shelves or inside a closet, and attack when they get there, a locked door or a ladder keeping them out for only two seconds, and guards and dungeon enemies standing next to the player turn to face them and attack instead of standing still. Large towns have more guards on patrol (Daggerfall has 48), and a patrolling guard can speak to a person in the street, buy food at a stall from a seller or tell a beggar to move, only when the beggar leaves; a guard says good morning only in the morning, and two patrols do not go to the same part of a town. In the open PvP zone the streets are not a safe zone for a villain: a player outside the streets can attack a villain in them, two guards come at once and one more every 30 seconds up to six, leaving when the villain leaves. Each guard has the villain's level and the villain's best attack skill, Critical Strike and best defence skill, and takes only 5% of the damage from each hit.
Townspeople ||| Townspeople in the living world run from a monster that comes close, along the street rather than through walls, and keep their distance. Those who stop to talk turn to face each other, a group waits until all have walked over and then takes turns, and the player hears them only then. People keep the same company about two minutes before moving on, with much less repetition, and indoors people at a table start talking as soon as they sit together. A stranger who said nothing as the player passed now speaks when the player stops in front of them, and a townsperson who stops for the player stays exactly where they stopped. They no longer stand inside walls or out over the harbour water or walk on the spot, and since 0.1.6640 they keep room from one another: people waiting at one spot keep a little distance, two leaving a shop together walk out in single file, and a walker steps aside, keeping right. Each square has several gathering points and every neighbourhood has a corner where its people meet, stall-keepers set up at the square or their own market, and people head out for the evening and the morning market at their own time. Guild members, priests, adventurers, sellswords and couriers walk the streets in their class, and someone keeping their place on their own appears as one of Daggerfall's own pictures of their kind, becoming themselves again the moment they walk on or join a conversation. A cat that meowed many times a second while the player looked at certain townspeople was fixed. Townsfolk inside a building spread through the whole room and sit at tables set apart, never at a table split by a wall, nobody waits at a tavern for hours for lunch, and everyone in a room sees the same people in the same places. In the street townsfolk fall out with each other, more often in the evening between drinkers, buyers haggle at stalls, beggars ask for a coin, and when someone shouts the people nearby fall quiet and turn to look. Small voices fill the street (the night watch calling the hour, stalls crying their wares, beggars calling out, drinkers singing home), every player in a town sees the same incidents, and beggars and stall keepers turn to face whoever they are talking to. Townsfolk inside a building spread through the whole room and sit at tables set apart, never at a table split by a wall, and everyone in a room sees the same people in the same places. In the street they fall out with each other (more often in the evening between drinkers), buyers haggle at stalls, beggars ask for a coin, and when someone shouts the people nearby fall quiet and turn to look. Small voices fill the street, every player in a town sees the same incidents, and Hammerfell's towns are walked by Redguards, who keep their names but get Redguard looks. Townsfolk walking into a shop are inside as soon as they reach the door, and nobody stands inside one another in a three-person conversation. How a person takes the player's tone changes what they think of them: townsfolk like plain speaking, so a Blunt question raises their regard and a Polite one lowers it. People who stop in the street look to the side or shift their weight, a beggar, labourer or fisher at work shows their own body rather than a still picture, people talking in a group look at the speaker, and a standing guard's axe is always visible. The new town lines show in the player's language when not playing in English.
Travellers ||| On coastal roads travellers no longer camp, fight or leave their fallen in the sea. New travellers set out from the towns: farmers leading a pack horse to market day, hunters, patrols of knights walking a round between a city and its region's towns, nobles travelling between courts with retainers and minstrels going to play another town's tavern, and bigger towns send out more pedlars, pilgrims and couriers. At night parties stop at a roadside inn when one is close ahead and otherwise make camp with a fire, parties meeting on the road greet each other, warn each other of trouble and share a camp, roads a patrol has walked recently see half the trouble, and travellers and caravans keep to the right of the road. Caravans and other road parties set off from outside the city walls instead of walking through them. Each town sends more travellers on the roads (adventurers, pedlars, pilgrims, couriers and hunters), the new adventurers travel in companies of their own and go into dungeons more often, and the roads have more parties each day, with more than three times as many adventurers going into dungeons.
Update Notice ||| A new version that comes out while the player plays is shown on screen, and installs when they quit or at once from File, Restart to Update. When a new version is available a line in chat and a banner say so; after one minute the game saves the character, shows a screen and loads the new version, waiting up to two minutes if the player is dead or in a duel, and Reload Now loads it at once.
Villain Meter ||| Killing a player in a PvP zone can raise the villain meter: a player 5 or more levels below adds to it and one without a PvP flag adds more, but a player who attacked the killer or party first adds nothing. At 5,000 the player is a villain with a red skull and the flag stays on, and the Stats page shows a Villainy bar to 10,000 with a red mark at 5,000. The meter stays with the character across logouts and areas, going down by 1 for a kill of a monster at the player's level or higher, 200 for a quest and 500 for a raid (a villain gets no decrease from a raid).
Weapon Swap ||| Outside the open PvP zone a weapon change takes half the time; in Cyrodiil, on the open sea and in their dungeons it does not change. The equip countdown outlasts the settle at the end of the swap pace's drain.
