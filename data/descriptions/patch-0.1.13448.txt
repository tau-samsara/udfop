Death Screen ||| The death screen was fixed and audited, including resolving the online screen's rise and ensuring quick-loading (F11) after dying in town no longer leaves the death screen up. It shows the amount of gold lost and says Rise instead of "End the journey". It names any letters of credit lost and what they were worth, and when the character's trading is frozen it says that what was dropped was lost outright. When a player kills the character the death screen shows that player's name.
Decorator ||| The decorator offers furnishings for homes. With the town mods on it adds 297 pieces such as beds and rugs, changing within a second when a town mod is turned on or off. It also offers what Daggerfall stands in its dungeons (thrones, cages, coffins, statues, chains, braziers) and a yard offers Daggerfall's street pieces and its own town's climate trees and plants, each with its own picture. People placed with the decorator (the Vendors list) can no longer be resized, with Smaller and Bigger gone for them. Those placed earlier at another size now stand at their normal size, moving one sets it to normal, an enlarged one gives back half the price difference and a shrunk one costs nothing to move. Decorations set against a wall sit in front of it instead of sinking into it, at any size and with the grid on, and the grid snaps along the wall. The furniture catalogue, the pieces in the room, the built-in pieces and the player's own things are laid out as picture tiles showing each piece's 3D model, name, price and badges for what it does, with a Pictures / List switch bringing the old lines back. Every tab, type, size and filter has its own icon, filters sit under named rows and fold behind one Filters button on phones, a placed piece's buttons are grouped by what they do with the costly Remove standing alone, and the placing bar is icon buttons with a Keys button. The catalogue puts each part in the category of what it is (plants in Trees and plants, dungeon tables and chairs in Furniture) and has four new categories, Statues, Tools, Animals and Remains. A large house holds more decorations than a small one, a door removed or put back in an online home shows so to all visitors, and a candle in a yard gives the same light as one in a house. Nudge left, right, away and back (or Numpad 4, 6, 8 and 2, Shift or Ctrl for a shorter distance) move a part to the side, one grid step when the grid is on, and a part can go a small distance into a wall; the roof does not stop decorations below it, only the ground floor's walls are the house, a lamp on a chain gives light from its flame, and a pale copy stays at a moved part's old position until it is put down.
Dwarven Dragon ||| The Dwarven Dragon is a Dwemer clockwork world boss that lands at different times in the open world and flies in the sky above its land, found when seen. It attacks the player who comes near, in the air and on the ground, with the ground showing where each attack will hit; after a Crash Landing it is on the ground for a short time when melee weapons hit it, while arrows and spells hit it at all times. When it dies it lies on the ground for ten minutes and each player who fought it receives a part of its treasure in the pack, more if they hit it, and the account keeps a record of the dragons killed (not a guest account), shown on the account and profile. Sometimes the core of a dead dragon keeps turning and the dragon can be tamed by a player who hit it, one per character; it goes into the pack and is ridden by selecting Dragon in the Transport window. On the ground it walks and gallops when the player runs, Jump flies it and in the air Jump climbs and Crouch dives. When the rider gets off it waits behind and is called with Dragon in the Transport window or the summon key, and on the Stable page it can be called, sent away and named. It has stats, a bond and hunger like a horse, drinks Dwarven Oil sold at each Stable, and flight and gallops use its breath, with no breath meaning it cannot take off and glides down in the air. Its saddlebags hold 1,000 kg. A rare blue dragon, the Aetherbright, has aetherium-blue brass and violet wings; an Admin on the dragon is seen by other players as a dragon, not a cart, and the dragon hovers with its wings beating and its body moving up and down when it stops in the air, as does a rider seen by others.
Gather Cards ||| A harvest shows as one card under the crosshair with the picture, +N name, STORES count, XP and bar, and gathering the same thing again adds to it. A gem, Heartwood, resin or hide's extra find shows as its own card, fish show their species, and a Motherlode strike is one bigger card with the ore, 10 silver, purse and miners struck. Silver gets a card wherever it is earned, and a muted card says so when the day's combat limit is reached. Cards for gathered items and silver stay 4 seconds longer: a gather or silver find card for 7.2 seconds and a Motherlode card for 9.
Gilded Weapons ||| The Dragonfire Oath is a Daedric Dai-Katana that can drop from the Warden of the Burning Gate about one fight in a hundred, giving +40% damage, +15 Strength, +30 Long Blade and +15 Endurance; its Dragonfire makes a blow that lands on a Daedra burn it again for another third of the hit and burn every other Daedra within 3 m for the same. The Sunken Sovereign is an Ebony Saber that can drop from the Old Coil about one kill in a hundred for a ship that did damage, giving +40% damage, +15 Agility, +30 Long Blade and 10% life leech; its Undertow adds +10% damage to each blow on the same foe up to +50%, fading after 6 seconds without a hit, and heals the player for 10% when it kills. Both are shown in the Collections codex with their own hints. The Item Maker can add enchantments to a Gilded weapon as to a Rare item, while its lines do not change and it keeps its name; one in hand shows a red glow, the color of its picture, also seen by other players.
Glyphs ||| Pressing a glyph on the account card hides it, and again shows it. Hiding only hides the icon, and anything the glyph lets the player do still works. On the enhanced skin the 2 and the Z are drawn so they can no longer be mistaken for each other. Origin is a $2 glyph, a dragon that bites its tail with a spark at its center and light moving on its scales. The Nirnroot Sage glyph, and the nirnroots of the Sage's Smoke aura, are a fan of seven thin sharp-edged leaves, teal at the center and pale green at the tips with pale veins.
Hand Wraps ||| Hand Wraps are a weapon for the fists: equipped in one hand, the player fights with both fists, punches use the Hand-to-Hand skill and do the usual fist damage, and the wraps add only what they carry (an enchantment, item lines, an imprint and a sigil). Enchanted wraps give the fists the same glow as an enchanted weapon, also on gloves and gauntlets and seen by other players. Weaponsmiths sell them and they can be found as loot.
Home Yards ||| Every home can put 60 decorations in its yard, also in a busy town, as a town's yards no longer fill up. Decorations near the player appear first and the game fetches only changes near the player rather than the full town each minute, and asks less often when nothing changes nearby; a yard change shows to other players near it in a few seconds, and decorations in other players' yards are drawn in groups for a higher frame rate in large cities. An older version of the game shows no yard decorations of other players, and a reload or restarting the desktop app updates it. In the Imperial City most houses with a court behind them have a yard, the part of the court behind the house; to put items in it the player stands at the house door and uses Decorate, where the game says where the yard is, and a house with a street on two sides has no yard. The border of a yard shows the line between it and each neighbor's yard, and with two houses next to each other no line divides their yards, each going to its full border.
Homes ||| Homes have a Who may enter setting, including My guild. Room furniture can be taken out, knightly order free houses are never owned by other players, graveyard crypts are not sold as homes online, and online homes/guild halls on the arena's block move to new houses on login without emptying storage. A phantom room tab in two-storey homes (often "Room 3") has been fixed. A home's chests no longer lose their contents when furniture fails to load, anything without a chest or box to go in being set down at the player's feet. When the servers are under heavy load the game waits longer between tries to load a town's homes, and loading a town's homes costs the servers half as many database requests. A home's furnishings load faster, especially when the server is busy. A home's crafting stations (forge, workbench, loom, mason's and jeweller's benches, alchemy and enchanting) can be used by the account's other characters as well as the buyer, while the storage still belongs to the character who bought it. In an online home Who may rest lets friends, the party or the guild rest there, and at the door of another player's home its decorations can be hidden for the viewer only.
Immersive Travel ||| Immersive Travel (by kkgobkk) places a carriage, horses and a driver outside the gates of walled cities. The player picks a town or village and pays a small daily fare to fast travel there, and carriages go to cities, hamlets and villages only, never putting the player on a ship. Disable Normal Travel is off by default and, when on, makes carriages the only fast travel, taking effect at the next start. Online a carriage is the way to fast travel over land, with the same carriages, fares and rules for everyone, and vampires and sunlight-hurt characters cannot take one by day. Carriage drivers take the player to towns in other provinces under the same rules and fare as in the Iliac Bay. The seven capitals outside the Iliac Bay each have a carriage and a driver at every road gate, used with Fast Travel as at the Bay's cities (the Imperial City carriage at the west end of Talos Bridge, Solitude's on level ground below the main gate), each gate of a walled city has a carriage, and the carriage, cart and horses stand off the road. With it on, the four city gates agree with the mod's own files.
Mods ||| The Mods pane was replaced by a unified system with every mod enabled, creator titles, one search across every tab, and a Mods & files section listing every mod with direct access to options. The mods for ship furniture and for underwater plants were removed, so the game always shows its own ships and plants.
PvP Combat Timer ||| After a hit between players a red ring around the PvP flag button empties over 60 seconds, with the seconds below it and a banner at the top of the screen, and the game says when the player is out of combat. A small panel at the bottom right shows the last three PvP hits. A Portal Stone cannot be used while the flag is on and the timer runs, the game saying how many seconds to wait, and resting in combat also says how many are left. A player who logs out during PvP combat in a PvP zone stays in the world, killable by other flagged players, until the timer stops; logging in again in that time returns the character at the same place and health, and if it was killed the next log-in shows the death screen naming the killer and wakes the player in the nearest town outside the zone.
PvP Flag ||| The PvP zones are Cyrodiil and the open sea, where Caps Lock or the button at the top right puts the PvP flag on or off from level 30. Without the flag attacks and spells do not hit players, so monsters can be fought safely near them; with it the player can attack all players, cannot remove it for a minute after a hit in a PvP fight, and without it can use a Portal Stone anywhere in the zone. Below level 30 players cannot attack or be attacked, and the game asks before going from level 29 to 30, where Not yet keeps level 29. A skull shows above a flagged player's name, white, gold for a minute after a PvP hit, red for a villain, also on the button and the Overworld view, and a flagged player has a red health bar below it with a thin blue line for a Shield spell, a hidden player showing a faint body, the skull and the bar but no name. With the flag up Levitate stops and cannot be cast, buffs stop and cannot be cast on oneself except Shield, and the player cannot rest, wait, ride a horse or a cart, while run speed and jump are limited to the best a character can have without magic and Paralyze does not hit a player. The level 30 message names the open Eltheric Ocean as a PvP zone, where it named all the sea. The flag can be turned on only in Cyrodiil, on the open sea and in their dungeons; elsewhere Caps Lock and the button do nothing, and leaving a zone with the flag on drops it when the combat timer stops. The button is at the middle of the right edge of the screen and shows a dark skull when the flag is off. A flagged player's health bar flashes when health is low, a flagged party member shows a green name and no skull, and at level 29 the level page shows Go to 30 when the player can.
PvP Spell Bar ||| The hot bar has a second set of slots for PvP, shown when the PvP flag is on in a PvP zone and replaced by the usual set when the flag goes off; keys 1 and 2 keep the potions, and the set cannot be changed during PvP combat. Only the spells on it can be cast with the flag on, each costing a part of maximum magicka and shown with its key and cost, grey when magicka is short. PvP spells on the hot bar or in the spellbook opens a small menu to pick a spell, its element and icon and select Make, then the spell is dragged onto a slot and removed by dragging it off or right-clicking the slot. The spells are Damage (an area spell with a chosen element), Heal, Shield, Resist, Reflect, Absorb, Fortify, Cure, Jump, Speed and Resurrect; a Heal or buff goes to the party member at the crosshair up to 15 m away, or to the player if none, and Resurrect costs 25% of maximum magicka and brings back a fallen party member whose body is near, not a player outside the party.
Sea Serpent ||| Every four hours Sethrakul the Old Coil, a great serpent, rises on a packet lane in open sea. Harbour bells ring fifteen minutes before, chat names the port and the map rings its waters, and players sail out and fight it with their ships' guns, each ship that joins making it tougher. It lashes with its tail, rams down a lane, bursts up under its mark and spits venom, from two-thirds health the Coil wraps a ship (broken by focus fire to stun it), and from one-third the Maelstrom drags ships toward a whirlpool's eye. It no longer teleports but swims every move, with a wake showing where it is going, and aims where the player's ship will be when an attack lands, surging at the player on the surface when out of reach and giving up after about 12 seconds of being outsailed. While exactly two ships fight it its attacks deal two thirds of their damage, so two ships can win, though one alone cannot in fair weather and three or more take full damage. A broadside is judged where the serpent was when the shot landed. A storm closes its waters fifteen minutes after it rises and ten minutes before it dives, every ship whose guns did a tenth of her share earns a hoard of gold and Magic-or-better pieces including a Deadlands Ember, and each serpent slain is worth six quests of Renown and pays 40 silver (20 for a ship standing by), toward the same 150 silver a day. Sethrakul's waters stay open until it dives, so the 08:00 storm no longer locks ships out, and the serpent has a sixth less health, so two ships at average gunnery win every time in about 8.5 minutes though one ship alone still cannot. Inside its waters a gale fills the sails from every direction and ships turn 1.5 times faster, its blows throw a ship half as far and tear half as much canvas, it is 1.6 times thicker so slow cannonballs fired with lag still land, and players on someone else's ship can hurt it with their own bows and attack spells. Ships have a wide invisible buffer and bounce off each other, rewards no longer depend on being near when it dies, a real part of the damage or a quarter of the fight within range earns a hoard, and party members of any ship that dealt heavy damage also get one if they came to the fight. Killing the serpent has a rare chance to grant the title Coilbreaker (1 in 20 kills) and the aura The Maelstrom (1 in 50), a three-armed whirlpool of tide and foam around the player's feet, kept for good and announced in a popup the first time. A ship that did damage may also win The Sunken Sovereign, about one kill in a hundred. If the server cannot give the reward for a sea serpent at once it tries again, where some players got none.
Ships ||| Ships feature carrack and large boat variations with gunports, wheels, steering, rigs, and swivel guns. Bank ship loans are removed and bug fixes cover audio and navigation. Far-off ship identification requires proximity, Lookouts, or spyglasses rather than automatic cards, and distant ships sailing around other players across the map are no longer built/animated on your screen. Ships far out at sea, including those other players leave behind on logging off, no longer keep a full animated hull far away and decide less often while nobody can see them, with their voyages, fights and damage carrying on as before. The ships' stern windows can be seen through by day (the galleon's into her great cabin, the carrack's transom windows) and close and glow warm at night when the lanterns are lit, while the caravan's windows stay see-through day and night. The galleon, carrack and large boat wear their own painted liveries again, with the oxblood strakes, the gilt and the stern gallery, in Daggerfall's own colour palette. Towns with ships in port run smoother online, the ships costing about a quarter of their old share of each frame and leaving far less garbage. Ship text no longer calls a ship "her" or "she" across the sea, the Fleet page, the shipyard, the crew hall, the Captain's Stars, the sea chart, the tutorials and the crew's chatter, saying "the ship", "this ship" or "it", and the 13 translated languages match. There is no stutter the first time a named or painted ship comes into view and painted sea ships no longer slow rendering. Ship parts already too heavy to take out of a chest or other storage are fixed when the save loads, with nothing in their hold lost, and a character too weak to carry a ship's parts is told "The ship's parts are too heavy for you to carry." The two buyable ships have new furniture: a cabin for the captain, beds for the crew, a mess, stores and a cargo hold, with crates and chests along the rails of the open deck, and each chest, crate, wardrobe and dresser below deck is safe storage as in a house. If a chest the player used is gone, or furniture now stands where items lay on the floor, the items are at the player's feet when going in, none lost.
Shipyards ||| Port towns have a shipyard on their seaward edge, its back to the water, where the shipmaster sells every ship Come Sail Away builds, from the Rowboat to the Carrack and the Large Galley, and buys the player's ships back. One window holds Ships for sale (each hull's size, crew, guns and price, with buying asking for confirmation), Your fleet (where each ship lies, her worth and what the shipmaster will pay, with Bring her here, lay up, rename and sell, a big sale paid as a letter of credit) and the Shipwright and Refits, which need Naval Combat on. The Rowboat goes into the pack as parts and every other ship's deed goes into the Fleet laid up at that port. The shipmaster will not buy a ship with cargo in her hold, anything in her cabin or her crew ashore with the player, or one that came into the realm through customs, and a sold ship's cabin furniture is sold too with half its value going to the bank. Townsfolk tell the player where the shipyard is and it is marked on the town map and as an anchor on the Overworld. Shipmaster cards show each ship's own 3D picture, and the Reforge, shipyard and naval-yard pages and a merchant's options each have their own icon. Towns away from the open sea, such as Ilessan Hills, no longer have a shipyard and a lighthouse beside a pond. The shipyard's fleet page reads properly again. The shipyard of Westhead Moor is on the north side of the town again, on the farmland at the north edge with Beautiful Cities and between the north wall and the sea without it, and the lighthouse moved.
Skyrim Aurora ||| On a clear night in Skyrim an aurora of green light with blue around it shows in the sky, bright and high in the north of Skyrim and less bright and low in the northern sky in the south. It starts after sunset and stops before sunrise, clouds can hide it and an overcast sky, rain or snow stops it, its strength changes each night and all players in the same place see the same one. At night it adds green light to the ground and calm water shows it. It can be seen only with the Enhanced Plus skin and Enhanced environments on.
Staff Panel ||| The Staff Panel is a tool for staff running the online game, not something players see. Its server side has cases, desk, players, realm, economy, census and status pages and a chat staff menu, and its relay half provides invisible follow, hidden staff, freeze, Message on screen and staff feeds (Network bursts, Monster watch and Bot check); version 0.2.3 added Set bank, a Banned tab, a Hide toggle, Me/Red chat and an in-game staff view. Following a player takes the camera into dungeons with them, shows the players around them as bodies and, from the chat menu, opens that player's card in the Staff Panel; monsters ignore hidden staff, and crowded player tags merge into one "N players here" chip. The staff pages answer in fewer database round trips. It can destroy every flagged copy of an item on a character in one act, remove extra realm-locked copies and unlock the ones a player keeps, leaves banned and trade-held gold out of the economy page, shows the total game worth, and counts a hidden staff member in no online total. The staff panel's heavy reads are kept for every isolate.
Underwater ||| The sea looks like water from below: blue-green, darker at depth and night, with the distance closing sooner. Sunbeams shine down through the water on sunny days, leaning with the sun and fading with depth. Each flooded part of a dungeon has kelp, coral, starfish, shells and weed on its floor, which can be swum through and give no cover or obstruction.
Villain Meter ||| Killing a player in a PvP zone can raise the villain meter: a player 5 or more levels below adds to it and one without a PvP flag adds more, but a player who attacked the killer or party first adds nothing. At 5,000 the player is a villain with a red skull and the flag stays on, and the Stats page shows a Villainy bar to 10,000 with a red mark at 5,000. The meter stays with the character across logouts and areas, going down by 1 for a kill of a monster at the player's level or higher, 200 for a quest and 500 for a raid (a villain gets no decrease from a raid). The villain bar is at the top of the Standing page, always there, and outside the zone shows the last value seen. A villain near the player shows as a red mark on the map.
Water Surface ||| The enhanced water surface covers oceans, rivers and ponds, with a basin, swell, foam and puddles that draw and swim. It is darker and less see-through, the shore is the art's own outline exactly and the water is the art's own, with shallows at 0.80 opacity and ?water=mask for debugging. At night water takes a quarter of a light diffusely and shows each light as a highlight, and it was audited. From high ground the far sea no longer fades into a pale band of fog ending in a hard square edge, but runs on through the haze to the horizon in one piece, with the same colour, light and sun glints as the water near the player and the sky reflected as the near water does, at no extra cost. Inland water banks (ponds, moats, rivers, streams and town canals) no longer show flat blue blotches on the grass, the shore's painted water sitting under the riverbed with the strip past the edge reading as dark wet mud, and a player on a bank can no longer see under the surface because the water's edge meets the ground. Water now shows when Enhanced environments is off and in the first moments after the game starts, where before it did not.
Weapon Hand ||| The raised blade was fixed. A weapon dropped on the L-Hand panel replaces only the left-hand weapon, or goes in the right hand if it cannot go in the left; a one-handed weapon alone in the left hand attacks, and Long Blade skill increases when attacking with a longsword, broadsword or saber in the left hand.
