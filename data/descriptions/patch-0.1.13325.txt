Abyss Dungeons ||| An Abyss Dungeon is the hardest dungeon in the game, online only. One at a time rises beside a busy city in the region with the most players online, with a column of brass-gold light over it, and the first player to reach its door finds it and everyone online hears where, after which its ring, compass mark, Timers row and notice-board note stay until it is gone. It has three foes at every spot, each four times as tough and hitting two and a half times as hard, six elite foes, half again the loot and no campfires. At its deepest point stands the Rift into the Shattered Hour, where the Orrery of Endings, the Unmoored Steps and the Brass Remnant are met, and the Remnant's fight, spoils, collapse and one-life rule are shared by everyone in the Hour at once. Each Abyss Dungeon keeps one of six Endings and two of nine omens, and fighters earn Hours broken toward the Hourbreaker title and The Turning Hour aura. Crew and sworn companions wait outside the Rift, and leaving the Hour for any reason puts the player on the ground at the dungeon's door. A rumour about a Hollow in a province gives the correct place, the game keeps a party's death and win in a Hollow outside the Bay, and in a party a province's Hollow does not replace the Iliac Bay copy the party uses. Past the Bay at most two new Abyss Dungeons are open at one time, and one open at the update stays open to its end.
Alinor ||| Alinor's Crystal Tower is no longer in the harbour; the Royal Palace stands on a terraced height at the top of a grand stair, and a river goes through the south of the city to the sea under two white bridges. The two bridges connect to the street at their ends with a ramp each, so the player can walk onto them, and townspeople do not walk through the ends.
Arena of Daggerfall ||| The Arena of Daggerfall is a colosseum in the heart of Daggerfall city, Kamer's Daggerfall Arena, used with his permission. It stands in every game with either city layout, is drawn with the player's own copy of Daggerfall's textures and dungeon pieces, and has walkable tiers and a floor. It replaced the tavern, two gem stores and houses on its block, whose occupants, quests, rooms and homes were moved or redirected elsewhere in the city. The arena help page names the two arenas.
Aura Armory ||| The Aura Armory puts light and fire on the parts of the body without changing the sprite, in five slots (Head, Torso, Arms, Legs and Weapon), one piece in each and any piece with any other, alongside the aura. Dagonfire pieces are sold at the Sigil Broker for 75 Deadlands Embers each (earned helping close Dagon's Breach) and Eternal pieces in the shop at $6 each or $20 for The Eternal Set; the pieces are the Crown of the Burning Door, Ember Heart and Ashbound Bracers, and the Eternal Sigil, Halo, Armlets and Greaves. A piece is worn from the profile's Character section by selecting a slot and a piece (again to remove it), with Wear at the Sigil Broker or on The Eternal Set in the shop, and the Collections page has an Aura Armory part. Pieces belong to the account, other players see changes at once, a weapon piece shows on each weapon in hand (an element shows first), and they show on Morrowind and sprite bodies, with the arms and legs, side-held weapons, staff head pieces and other players' names above head pieces fixed. Others' Aura Armory in Features set to All, Near or None shows fewer pieces of other players, and the player's own always show. The player's own pieces show on the character in the world, the weapon piece on the weapon, and other players see a newly worn piece at once; the Eternal cape and the Holo Shadow Cloak cover the pieces under them when seen from behind.
Bounty Hunts ||| A bounty hunt is the fight a notice sends the player to. An open-ground notice marks a land spot 4 to 10 map pixels from the town with a black circle, and walking onto it summons 4 to 8 monsters whose kinds depend on the player's level, from Rats and Giant Bats up to Gargoyles, Wraiths and Daedroths. A real dungeon 4 to 10 pixels away can instead be entered, and a farm notice builds a farmstead copied from a nearby farm. A pack of 6 to 8 is hunted in two groups, and if the pack despawns the kills are kept and only survivors return. A bounty completed in a region can give influence to the seat of that region, if the player's war guild pledges to that seat and the player has been a member for 7 days or more; at most 10 bounties a week give influence. A bounty at a graveyard puts its monsters at the graveyard and messages the player when they are near, and in a party the bounty continues when a member goes into the crypt.
Cloaks ||| Casual and Formal Cloaks are now real cloaks worn over your armour instead of a full robe that hid it, hanging from the shoulders and swinging with your legs as you walk with smooth shoulders and individual colours for each dye. The Cloaks panel in the inventory has a Show cape control that hides the cloak and the aura, also for other players, and shows them again when selected again; a hidden cloak still protects from cold and rain, and the Aura Armory pieces do not change.
Death Screen ||| The death screen was fixed and audited, including resolving the online screen's rise and ensuring quick-loading (F11) after dying in town no longer leaves the death screen up. It shows the amount of gold lost and says Rise instead of "End the journey". It names any letters of credit lost and what they were worth, and when the character's trading is frozen it says that what was dropped was lost outright. It does not name the Iliac Bay.
Docks ||| Port towns' docks now appear even with Naval Combat or Come Sail Away turned off, and always connect to the town, with a pier running to land when Deep Waters is on. Ports with docks some distance from the town, such as Daggerfall, Wayrest and Pencart, show their docks on arrival by sea. The ground near Kalunnunu, and other high towns on the coast, rises to meet the town, so the docks are not below a cliff.
Family Tab ||| The Family tab is part of Project Legacy, in the pause window between Holdings and System. It has the Tree (zoom, pan, play as a sibling, pass the mantle, and where each member lives), the House (model, seat, generations, the living and the fallen, and the family's homes) and the Hall of Ancestors (every house founded), and keypad / opens it. The character sheet on the Stats tab also shows the house, its model and the player's generation and, in an Enduring house, the age against the lifespan, the years Arkay has taken and when the player becomes an elder. A child of two married player characters shows on both parents' family trees and stays a member of the house where it was born.
Features ||| Features is the screen where every enhanceable feature is switched. It is a panel of tiles with on and off shown as green and red, an All off button and a search. Descriptions are in plain words, and Daggerfall Unity's own options end with their default, such as "Off by default in Daggerfall Unity." Under Combat, a Steel Helm switch picks Closed (a visor and a blue plume, the default) or Open (a nasal helm with a mail aventail that shows the face). Snowfall, Windfall and Heat Haze each have a switch, on by default in the enhanced outdoors, while the desert sand and winter ground are packs of their own on the Replacement packs card. The Tamriel on the map and Land beyond the Bay rows are on by default on the enhanced skin. The Features panel says what is in the land outside the Iliac Bay and that fast travel goes there when Land beyond the Bay is on (online fast travel does not go into or out of Cyrodiil), and the help pages, crew, sea news and other lines no longer name the Iliac Bay when the player can be in any province.
Hearth Offerings ||| A Hearth Offering is set out at the door of a home during the Witches' Festival and filled with Soul Cakes. Other players can take one cake from each offering per day, and the owner can take it back in at any time, even after the festival, with the bowl and every cake still in it. When other players have taken 25 cakes from a player's offerings the player earns the Hearthkeeper title, and taking 10 cakes from others' offerings earns the Soul Cake icon. Interacting in a town with an offering no longer crashes the game, and the names under the crosshair show again. The Hearth Offering at a door is a carved pumpkin on a stool below a lantern. All characters on an account can put the Hearth Offering at the door of the house, and it has no weight.
Helm Panel ||| At the helm a panel shows the ship's hull, sails, crew, guns reloading, waters and notoriety, and the ship's speed through the water in knots beside her name. It shows the controls at a glance with a sails button that uses the arrows, and during an Overworld sea crossing the panel and sail keys stay out of the journey's way. On a phone or tablet it has its own Brace button, holds in two rows of helm buttons and sits above the touch buttons with tap hints. When the load makes the ship slow, the helm panel, and a passenger, shows "Too heavy. Lighten your pack, your wagon or the hold".
Home Yards ||| Every home can put 60 decorations in its yard, also in a busy town, as a town's yards no longer fill up. Decorations near the player appear first and the game fetches only changes near the player rather than the full town each minute, and asks less often when nothing changes nearby; a yard change shows to other players near it in a few seconds, and decorations in other players' yards are drawn in groups for a higher frame rate in large cities. An older version of the game shows no yard decorations of other players, and a reload or restarting the desktop app updates it. In the Imperial City most houses with a court behind them have a yard, the part of the court behind the house; to put items in it the player stands at the house door and uses Decorate, where the game says where the yard is, and a house with a street on two sides has no yard.
Homes ||| Homes have a Who may enter setting, including My guild. Room furniture can be taken out, knightly order free houses are never owned by other players, graveyard crypts are not sold as homes online, and online homes/guild halls on the arena's block move to new houses on login without emptying storage. A phantom room tab in two-storey homes (often "Room 3") has been fixed. A home's chests no longer lose their contents when furniture fails to load, anything without a chest or box to go in being set down at the player's feet. When the servers are under heavy load the game waits longer between tries to load a town's homes, and loading a town's homes costs the servers half as many database requests. A home's furnishings load faster, especially when the server is busy. A home's crafting stations (forge, workbench, loom, mason's and jeweller's benches, alchemy and enchanting) can be used by the account's other characters as well as the buyer, while the storage still belongs to the character who bought it. More of the furniture in houses of the town mods, such as in Alinor, can be removed, with walls, beams and pillars staying, and the beds from the town mods point the correct way.
Imperial City ||| The Imperial City stands on City Isle in the middle of Lake Rumare, 25 map pixels (about 20 km) wide, with a round White-Gold Tower 1,360 m tall rising from the Emperor's round palace, a 38 m wall with six watch towers and nine gates, Green Emperor Way and three ring roads. Its districts (Market, Arena District, Arboretum, Temple District, Talos Plaza, Elven Gardens, Waterfront and the Palace District) each have their own style and streets, the roofs can be crossed, and most buildings have named Daggerfall interiors that match their outside. Every door shows its name, the stairs are built stone, the Imperial Prison is on the north-east shore, and lamp-posts light the streets. Lake Rumare is part of the sea joined to the Niben through a canal, so a ship can sail up to the Waterfront, which is a port with passage to and from Senchal and Torval. Travel in its streets goes up to 60 times walking speed, and the districts are made ahead of the player on background workers. A house's yard in the Imperial City no longer goes into other houses and the street, and items put near a house before should be removed standing at its door.
Item Insurance ||| A magic or crafted item can be insured outside the open PvP zone with Insure on its card in the pack, for a fifth of its value, starting after two minutes. If a player kills the owner the killer cannot take an insured item, the insurance ends only when it saves the item, and a death never takes the cargo of the player's cart or the companions' packs. If every worn item of a killed player is insured, the killer gets the gold of one policy (a letter of credit if too heavy) and the victim keeps the item while the policy ends; the insurance of a villain killed by a player keeps no item. All gear can be insured in the open PvP zone, weapons, armor, shields, jewelry and clothing, common and old items too, by opening the item's card outside the zone and selecting Insure. A policy costs 20% of the item's value, plus the gold for any enchantments the item maker put on it.
Keybindings ||| The keybinding standard (KB1) was introduced and audited, and shared keys were reworked in 0.1.4122. A key that is set but not confirmed says "Not saved yet - press Confirm." on its row, the top of the page counts the unsaved changes and highlights Confirm, and the Confirm bar no longer hides behind the Settings search bar or covers the row being bound on a phone. Polling a bound key no longer builds a list for every binding. Swing Weapon bound to Left Click, with mouse activate unbound, survives a restart. When a key is assigned that is already in use, the confirmation appears beside the binding being changed instead of at the top of the controls list. A key held when the player changes to a different window is released, also on a controller.
Launch Speed ||| The first launch after an update no longer freezes for half a minute while the game prepares its graphics, as the work runs in the background while the world loads, and the first seconds of play no longer stop while effects such as weapon glows and foe warnings get ready. On a slower or busy computer the volumetric clouds no longer stop the first launch, the sky showing simpler clouds until the full ones are ready, and the pictures of the character's body load in the background. The game keeps some results of the first launch (town ground, ship models and pictures, shipyard and lighthouse sites, and the map of Tamriel), using about 9 MB of storage, which makes the first launch a little slower and later launches' pauses in Daggerfall shorter. After logging in, the game prepares effect shaders one at a time instead of all at once, which removed a freeze of 10 to 15 seconds on some computers, though other players' auras can appear some seconds after the players. After an update the water and rain graphics prepare in the background, so the game does not stop for some seconds on entering the world, and the desktop app removes its old graphics cache after each update to prevent a slow start. After an update the game starts making its world graphics on the main menu, where a bar at the bottom left shows the progress and says Graphics ready when full, so Chrome no longer stops for up to a minute on entering the world; play can start at any time. The bar shows each time the main menu opens, also when the graphics were ready before it opened.
Main Menu ||| The main menu has Install beside Play, a profile mark that is a portrait of the last character, and Patreon and Ko-fi icons in the top-left corner that open their pages in the browser. It is no longer cut off on short windows, and the logo sits below the corner icons on narrow windows. The buttons are carved stone slabs in the Enhanced Plus style, all one width, and on phones they are a little more compact so the whole menu fits above the Screenshots and About buttons. Main menu clicks play the in-game click sound once the game data is loaded. The main menu first asks one question, Online or Offline, and Offline opens Continue, New Game, Load Game and the Test Room with a way back. Settings and Overhauls are in the bottom bar beside Screenshots and About, which no longer covers the buttons on phones, small or short screens and long languages. Double-clicking Offline no longer opens Load Game by accident, Escape puts the keyboard back on the button the player came from, and Tutorials and Screenshots are translated. In Chrome and Edge on Windows and Mac a Fix stutter button shows the steps to delete the browser's old graphics files, which the game cannot delete, the first load afterwards being slower once; the graphics bar shows each time the menu opens and says Graphics ready when full.
Maps ||| New setting: Settings → Interface → Maps → Map text and icons - Auto, or 0.75× to 2×. With Map Backgrounds on, the character sheet's background in a province shows the town, dungeon or graveyard rather than the race's background.
Online Characters ||| Creating an online character starts the game directly, and server-side save auditing checks items, attributes, and wealth growth against playtime while safeguarding player progress. The Online page shows the families that have no living character, and Remove family removes one to make space for a new family without changing the player's characters.
Open PvP Zone ||| Cyrodiil is the open PvP zone, exactly the province with its lakes and rivers, and the red line, fog and edge of the zone map follow its border; the map shows the zone offline too, with its fog, red line and rings, and its city and town names. The streets of each city, town and village are a safe zone where no player can hit or cast at the player, and a message tells when the player enters or leaves; the open land, roads, dungeons, halls, farms, manors, inns and temples are not safe. A monster's kill returns the player to a town of Cyrodiil in the same ring and a player's kill to the nearest town outside the zone. For a minute after a hit between players items cannot be put into storage, a second kill by the same player within 30 minutes takes nothing, and for 60 seconds after entering or coming back to life no player can hurt the player, ended by attacking a player or taking items from remains. The Wrothgarian Mountains are a usual region again, and the Lake Rumare and Niben inside the province are part of the zone. Since 0.1.13148 a death in a PvP zone returns the player to a town and gives no safe passage on return, safe passage coming only from walking across the zone's edge, not by teleporting or leaving a town or dungeon. Killing a player gives one random worn item that is not insured, and the victim does not drop items in the bag. The streets are not safe for a villain: a villain can be attacked from the streets, cannot attack back from them, and the town guards do not come when a villain is attacked there. In the zone a Recall, a guild teleport or a teleport to the party goes only into the streets of a town, where before a Mark near the walls could send the player into the open country; with the PvP flag off a Portal Stone takes the player into and out of Cyrodiil's open country; and leaving a building gives no safe passage. A PvP death does not drop the bag: the killer can take one uninsured worn item, or gold, and the zone messages, map, tutorial and insurance text say so.
Province Peoples ||| Each province past the Iliac Bay has its own people, most persons in Skyrim being Nords, in Morrowind Dark Elves and in Cyrodiil Imperials, with some persons from neighbours (Imperial soldiers and traders in Morrowind, Khajiit in Valenwood) and some areas of a different people (Reachmen in the Reach, many Khajiit in Reaper's March, many Imperials in Gideon, many Argonians and Khajiit in Leyawiin). Persons in the street, residents, keepers, port crews, guards and enemies all follow these rules and take a name from their own people. Inside buildings more persons now show a body of the correct race, in the Bay too, and some persons past the Bay have a new name and race after the update, though a courtship already started keeps its name and race. A person or yard figure near a province border has the race of the land where it stands.
PvP Flag ||| The PvP zones are Cyrodiil and the open sea, where Caps Lock or the button at the top right puts the PvP flag on or off from level 30. Without the flag attacks and spells do not hit players, so monsters can be fought safely near them; with it the player can attack all players, cannot remove it for a minute after a hit in a PvP fight, and without it can use a Portal Stone anywhere in the zone. Below level 30 players cannot attack or be attacked, and the game asks before going from level 29 to 30, where Not yet keeps level 29. A skull shows above a flagged player's name, white, gold for a minute after a PvP hit, red for a villain, also on the button and the Overworld view, and a flagged player has a red health bar below it with a thin blue line for a Shield spell, a hidden player showing a faint body, the skull and the bar but no name. With the flag up Levitate stops and cannot be cast, buffs stop and cannot be cast on oneself except Shield, and the player cannot rest, wait, ride a horse or a cart, while run speed and jump are limited to the best a character can have without magic and Paralyze does not hit a player. The level 30 message names the open Eltheric Ocean as a PvP zone, where it named all the sea. The flag can be turned on only in Cyrodiil, on the open sea and in their dungeons; elsewhere Caps Lock and the button do nothing, and leaving a zone with the flag on drops it when the combat timer stops. The button is at the middle of the right edge of the screen and shows a dark skull when the flag is off.
Raids ||| A town saved from raiders stays saved when the world's clock is corrected and pays the player once, and guards and townspeople are safe from the player's blade and horse during a raid. Raids are named 15 minutes before they start, including raids early in the game day, and show on the World Events board. Raids come in kinds: Assault (waves with a pause between, the last wave the party's leader with two boss traits), Siege of the Standard (keep the Standard-Bearer alive), Exodus (guide three refugees to the square) and the classic Raid. A town that is held gives more reputation, half again the gold and one extra Magic-or-better item. Raiders attack towns in each province outside the Iliac Bay except Cyrodiil, with each province's own raids each day made of the people and monsters living there; a message arrives when the player is in the same district, and the world map shows a town raiders attack. The game tells the player about a raid outside the Iliac Bay, and when joining the world a raid that players cleansed does not show as active; the Danish and Norwegian text for the raids setting is correct.
Real Windows ||| Twelve more types of house window are see-through, showing the room behind them from the street and the street through them from inside. Window light follows the Real windows setting, and with it Off windows give no light.
Roads ||| A road crash (ROAD-CRASH) was fixed, and roads are painted onto new terrain about five times faster. Roads and tracks no longer climb every hill. Camps, bounty farms and trail spots are not on the roads outside the Iliac Bay, and the roads on the Iliac Bay that cross the sea are now dry, where before water covered them and the player fell through.
Sea Serpent ||| Every four hours Sethrakul the Old Coil, a great serpent, rises on a packet lane in open sea. Harbour bells ring fifteen minutes before, chat names the port and the map rings its waters, and players sail out and fight it with their ships' guns, each ship that joins making it tougher. It lashes with its tail, rams down a lane, bursts up under its mark and spits venom, from two-thirds health the Coil wraps a ship (broken by focus fire to stun it), and from one-third the Maelstrom drags ships toward a whirlpool's eye. It no longer teleports but swims every move, with a wake showing where it is going, and aims where the player's ship will be when an attack lands, surging at the player on the surface when out of reach and giving up after about 12 seconds of being outsailed. While exactly two ships fight it its attacks deal two thirds of their damage, so two ships can win, though one alone cannot in fair weather and three or more take full damage. A broadside is judged where the serpent was when the shot landed. A storm closes its waters fifteen minutes after it rises and ten minutes before it dives, every ship whose guns did a tenth of her share earns a hoard of gold and Magic-or-better pieces including a Deadlands Ember, and each serpent slain is worth six quests of Renown and pays 40 silver (20 for a ship standing by), toward the same 150 silver a day. Sethrakul's waters stay open until it dives, so the 08:00 storm no longer locks ships out, and the serpent has a sixth less health, so two ships at average gunnery win every time in about 8.5 minutes though one ship alone still cannot. Inside its waters a gale fills the sails from every direction and ships turn 1.5 times faster, its blows throw a ship half as far and tear half as much canvas, it is 1.6 times thicker so slow cannonballs fired with lag still land, and players on someone else's ship can hurt it with their own bows and attack spells. Ships have a wide invisible buffer and bounce off each other, rewards no longer depend on being near when it dies, a real part of the damage or a quarter of the fight within range earns a hoard, and party members of any ship that dealt heavy damage also get one if they came to the fight. Killing the serpent has a rare chance to grant the title Coilbreaker (1 in 20 kills) and the aura The Maelstrom (1 in 50), a three-armed whirlpool of tide and foam around the player's feet, kept for good and announced in a popup the first time. A ship that did damage may also win The Sunken Sovereign, about one kill in a hundred. The release of 0.1.13231 moved its place on each day one time, and new sea lanes no longer move it.
Ship Handling ||| Ships sail on every heading. Pointed straight into the wind a ship makes a little over half her best speed, where before she stalled or was blown backwards, and she is fastest with the wind behind her off to one side, with no more tacking. Ships are faster under sail (a galleon with the wind on her side nearly two thirds faster), a storm drives her harder up to twice a fair breeze, and in fog she keeps nearly a third of her speed. They pick up speed in about 7 seconds instead of 25, slow to boarding speed in about 8, and turn on a wider circle at the faster speed. The rudder works at a standstill and bites hardest at half sail, rowing after striking sail no longer holds speed, and AI ships turn and gather speed at the player's own ship's pace. Classic handling is one switch away in Features, Naval Combat, Ship handling. Ships no longer stick on rocks or in place at sea, and one run aground can float off. With no enemies near, no ship chasing and no boarding or aiming going on, a ship's sails and oars give 15% more speed. A parked wagon or one at a stable adds no weight to the ship, only a wagon that stays with the player adds its load, and the player cannot mount a horse or wagon on the deck, where Summon and Send away do not operate. A boat with too much cargo can stop its turn and its movement, and a turn stops quickly when the helm is turned the other way.
Spawned Dungeons ||| Spawned dungeons are ported and expire, 2 in-game days when cleared and 7 in general. They are announced on their own pixel in metres, appear only where the ground is dry land above the beach line and no longer on beaches or at sea, and pixels that once held a shoreline spawn are now empty land or sea for everyone. Random dungeons in the online wilderness are a quarter fewer; one that remains is the same place as before. Everyone stands on the same ground around one, whether the dungeon is still around or has expired, and an expired one leaves its level clearing and ground tiles until it comes back. Online, spawned dungeons are in all of Tamriel, some of them Elite as in the Bay, and the holes in the ground past the Bay where a spawned dungeon stood on the land were repaired. Two dungeons outside the Iliac Bay do not share their resources; each has its own.
Timers ||| The Timers window, opened by an hourglass by the profile icon online, shows shared events with live countdowns in the player's own clock: Dagon's Breach (when the next opens, and when a standing one seals and collapses), town raids in the region, the next raid elsewhere and how many come today, the Sunday Turning at 18:00 UTC and the Friday 18:00 UTC Reckoning when pledges lock, this week's sieges, tourneys, revolts and any Royal Tourney, the Season's end, new bounty hunts and raids rolling, and the daily reset at 00:00 UTC. It also counts down the sea serpent's rising, storm and dive and shows the next rising. Events running now are listed first, and tapping outside, Escape or B closes it. The window is grouped into World events, The Battlespire, Town raids, Seats and battles, and Resets, each heading showing how many of its events are under way. A bar of buttons at the top shows one group at a time (or All), each with its count and a green mark when something in it is live, and the choice is kept until the game is closed. An event under way says Now above its countdown. The window keeps one height and only its list scrolls, buttons are thumb-sized on a phone, and Elite Breach rows wear the Breach's gold and Dagon's Breach rows its red. The Timers window shows the two provinces for this game day and for the next two.
Travel Map ||| The travel map is a parchment held in the player's hands, drawn as a held sprite with a Features switch, a Morrowind map on every sheet, readable glyphs and a fourth painting in the hands. A default trip is walked, the map was audited several times, and players riding another's boat are marked on the map where their boat is. The Overworld travel view no longer leaves 1 to 1.7 GB of extra memory behind after a journey, and it draws faster while a route is on screen. Clicking near a town walks around it and clicking its middle goes in, and with "travel only to places" on, clicking anywhere on a town travels to it. A place past the Iliac Bay can be clicked on the travel map to open the travel panel, and Begin journey walks there on the overworld (a walk, not fast travel), also to open land when Travel Options lets the player travel to a spot, but a click on the sea starts no journey. The character goes to the gate of a city, dungeon or quest location rather than the center of its map square, and to cities on the coast such as Solitude, which the game used to stop with a message about the water. A waypoint on land past the Bay shows Travel here, one on the water does not, and the travel map now takes the player to cities, towns, villages and other places in all provinces as in the Bay. The travel map shows no black line in the sea at the west edge of the Iliac Bay, and a place past the Bay shows when its gates, raids, stars and Abyss Dungeons start.
Travel Options ||| Travel options set how journeys run. Fare scaling counts the player's Mercantile, so a good haggler pays less, a journey stops for a hostile ship including a crossing by sea, and changing the settings takes effect straight away. A Player Controlled Inns Travel dial, on by default, makes any trip stopping at inns a journey, and online every trip over land on the travel map is travelled, not instant, with its options always on. Two speed dials returned in 0.1.6646 after being removed in 0.1.6444: one for the journey's speed and one for the speed while enemies are near, each going x5, x10, x20, x30, x40 and x60, with x100 on a road only. Leaving a road drops a dial at x100 to x60 at once and it does not climb back by itself, journeys start at x60, and the dials are in every interface (the Overworld's panel, the Enhanced journey bar, and a small speed box with the classic interface and GrimoireUI). It can skip ahead on a journey outside the Iliac Bay.
Villain Meter ||| Killing a player in a PvP zone can raise the villain meter: a player 5 or more levels below adds to it and one without a PvP flag adds more, but a player who attacked the killer or party first adds nothing. At 5,000 the player is a villain with a red skull and the flag stays on, and the Stats page shows a Villainy bar to 10,000 with a red mark at 5,000. The meter stays with the character across logouts and areas, going down by 1 for a kill of a monster at the player's level or higher, 200 for a quest and 500 for a raid (a villain gets no decrease from a raid). The villain bar is at the top of the Standing page, always there, and outside the zone shows the last value seen.
Wind ||| Wind has wisps, a quiet wind loop, flora that sways, wind seen and heard off one mapping, and the sky's direction. With Enhanced sounds on, outdoor wind is a soft steady rush rising and falling, not a repeated moan. The Sway setting moves both grass and trees, and turning it off holds both still. Wind changes from day to day: trees and plants sway and shiver, some days turn windy or stormy, and gusts come and go with their own sound. Leaves blow off the trees, in winter snow blows off the boughs, and the wind waits while the game is paused. A ship makes way under sail after the player goes into a building and comes out again; the wind sometimes stayed at zero, which stopped every ship under sail while oars still worked.
Window Light ||| At night the light of a lit room goes through its window onto the street, the window frame and bars casting shadows in it, while a dark room gives no light and a curtain makes the light on the ground smaller. In a building the sunlight and moonlight go through the windows onto the floor and furniture, moving with the time of day and changing with the weather, with walls and furniture casting shadows in the sunlight with Enhanced Lighting on; by day the rooms seen through windows from the street also have sunlight on their floors.
World Events ||| World events are the timed events such as the Burning Court, the Shattered Hour, the Spire and the Sky-Ketch. Regeneration works in them at half strength, and not at all for 5 seconds after a hit, and party members can be resurrected. On the Enhanced HUD a tile on the status row names the event the player is in and shows its rules and when regeneration resumes. Past the Bay only two provinces start Oblivion Gates, town raids, fallen stars and Abyss Dungeons on each game day, each province taking its turn on one of every four game days, and a game day has three Oblivion Gates, not nine; the Iliac Bay keeps all its events each day, and the Sky-Ketch and sea serpent do not change.
World Map ||| Every port the player has discovered shows an anchor at every zoom, including the whole-bay view, drawn darker with a light outline so it stands out on a busy coast. The Enhanced world map opens on the player as if Find me were pressed, gliding to where they stand with the red cross blinking, while the classic map is unchanged. Online, pointing at open ground shows a card listing the ores its veins can be, what its dungeons' deep veins hold, its kingdom's own ore and whether the ground is witnessed yet. The Find me button is back on the world and town maps. Opening the map for the first time in a session takes 0.7 s on a desktop instead of 6.7 s. The world map is titled Tamriel when it shows all of Tamriel, a place outside the Iliac Bay no longer shows "(beyond the Bay)", and a dungeon outside the Bay does not show until the player finds it or a quest names it.
