Account Card ||| The account card shows a player's registered date and time played, and a row of Renown for each of their characters. The level beside the player's name is that of the last character played. The profile has a Character section showing the character with the aura, Aura Armory pieces, and title and glyphs above the head, with slots on the two sides (Head, Torso, Arms, Legs, Over the head, Weapon, Portrait and Aura); pointing at a choice shows it on the character, selecting wears it, and a piece the player does not have can be tried on with text saying how to get it. On a phone the account window fills the screen.
Accounts ||| Accounts hold a player's login and their cloud saves, served by an account Worker that issues tokens and checks passwords. The service was built in 0.1.3607 and 0.1.3690, then repaired after an outage caused by an overly costly password-hashing setting on Cloudflare, and later given a clock fix. The account server has since been protected from overload: after it fell over again in 0.1.6625 games now wait longer after each failed ask instead of retrying at once, and in 0.1.6640 signing in, joining a room and loading a town's house layouts were made far cheaper for it. The letterbox, the count over a town's Notice Boards and the play time now share one request to the account service instead of three, cutting its database work by about a third per player. A username can no longer be an email address: a player who signed up with one still signs in with it but other players see a generated name. After a server update players no longer all reconnect at once, each game waiting a moment (longer the more players are online), sign-in token requests and the account, realm, heartbeat and profession reads are answered cheaply from read copies of the database, the letterbox, Notice Board count and play time share one request, and when many players sign in at once they wait in a short line instead of getting a "server" error. Most menus that talk to the server (the Guild tab, the market, the Seat tab, letters, the account card and signing in) load much faster, since the account server now runs next to its database instead of across the Pacific, and joining the world, the chat's player list and the friends list come up sooner after signing in. When the servers are overloaded the game holds off on loading homes, yards, seats, traders, guild invitations, Marks and the save list and asks again later, waiting longer the longer the overload lasts, while actions such as buying, crafting, saving and claiming still go through, and the servers also turn those requests away for a moment to recover. Town homes, rooms and decorations, gathering nodes, traders, Marks, guild invites, lineages, unions, cloud saves, the Arena board and the town seats list now load from read copies of the database instead of the one main database. Logging in, saving and the town, seat and notice-board panels stay responsive at peak hours, as several of the busiest requests ask the account database far fewer times, and when the service is overloaded the game backs off its background checks (mail, the notice board, the day's motherlodes) instead of asking again at once. The account server does less work per player, so sign in and saves fail less when many players are online.
Aura Armory ||| The Aura Armory puts light and fire on the parts of the body without changing the sprite, in five slots (Head, Torso, Arms, Legs and Weapon), one piece in each and any piece with any other, alongside the aura. Dagonfire pieces are sold at the Sigil Broker for 75 Deadlands Embers each (earned helping close Dagon's Breach) and Eternal pieces in the shop at $6 each or $20 for The Eternal Set; the pieces are the Crown of the Burning Door, Ember Heart and Ashbound Bracers, and the Eternal Sigil, Halo, Armlets and Greaves. A piece is worn from the profile's Character section by selecting a slot and a piece (again to remove it), with Wear at the Sigil Broker or on The Eternal Set in the shop, and the Collections page has an Aura Armory part. Pieces belong to the account, other players see changes at once, a weapon piece shows on each weapon in hand (an element shows first), and they show on Morrowind and sprite bodies, with the arms and legs, side-held weapons, staff head pieces and other players' names above head pieces fixed. Others' Aura Armory in Features set to All, Near or None shows fewer pieces of other players, and the player's own always show.
Auras ||| Auras are cosmetic effects worn from the aura picker on the account card, each granted with a title. They include the Oblivion Ward, Golden Radiance, the Holo Shadow Cloak (with the Shadow Fang title), the developers' Seraph Wings and Crystal Resonance (with the Crystal Fist title). An aura now shows to everyone nearby within a second or two, up to 32 are drawn at once, and the cloak and wings fold away while riding or swimming. They sit correctly on both Morrowind bodies and Eye of the Beholder sprites. Lilac Warren is SylviaBun's aura: six lilac bunnies with cotton tails and little maid's headdresses hop round the player's feet on a ring of lilac light, leaving fading paw-print tracks, with an occasional happy binky and sparkles of lilac, pink and white. The Elite Breach adds Beaconlight (Redoubt 10), The Burning Stair (Redoubt 20) and Firstlight. Eight auras gained detail: Dagon's Fire (cracks of fire in the ring's stone, sparks and higher flames), The Golden Radiance (two ribbons turning round its column and a twelve-point sun below the feet), The Maelstrom (light moving on the water's bottom and a ring of foam), Beaconlight (a beam up from each lit beacon), The Burning Stair (cracks of fire and small flames on its steps), Eternal (the mark of the Origin and a ring of fire below the feet), Witchfire (a seven-point star between its candles, each with a flame) and The Crystal Resonance (a small square above each column that falls, and on each beat a ring of squares moving out). On the female Acrobat cloak and wing auras stay on the shoulders when seen from an angle, and other players' auras can appear some seconds after the players after logging in.
Bounty Boards ||| Bounty boards are notice boards in towns with two or more notice boards, where half become Bounty Boards. Every board in a town posts the same four hunts to every player, and notices change each in-game day. Pressing one opens the board window in the Enhanced Plus style. Clicking Take bounty repeatedly takes the board's notices in order from the top, after the last take the notice shows a short line instead of buttons, Give up asks for a second click and sits on the left, and the board keeps one size so the Take button stays put. The hunt record on the bounty board updates every ten minutes.
Character Creation ||| Character creation is the screen where a new character is made. The creator tells the player what they rolled, a phone version exists, and a character made online no longer gets a skill check the moment it is created. The Project Legacy question (Enduring, Bloodline, No lineage) and the leveling question are Enhanced Plus popups in which the option under the pointer is the one highlighted and clicking it selects it. Additionally, heads and hairs are now paired freely, from every pairing of the heads and hairs your Morrowind data offers a race at character creation, whereas there were previously ten. Classes are grouped as Magic, Stealth and Combat and each shows the two attributes it relies on. The class card shows the class's attributes, skills and health per level before its description, with health reading as the real range (for example "3-6 a level, more with Endurance"). On phones "Begin your life" and "Play as..." stay on screen, the province names on the map no longer overlap, the drums play only when a class is chosen, Start over sits beside Back, and buttons say "Play as an Acrobat". Small text is at least 12px. With Morrowind's data attached, the Face step says the portraits could not be loaded only when the game cannot draw a portrait, and it starts drawing the faces when the name is chosen.
Collections ||| Holdings > Collections is open to every player in six parts: Titles, Auras, Icons, Glyphs, Codex and Cards. Every title, aura, profile icon and glyph is listed, held or not, grouped by where it comes from, with a tile showing how many are held of the total and a row saying whether it can be had now (Obtainable, By chance, Ranked, Seasonal, Patron reward and so on). Rows open to say how to get it, can be filtered by All, Held, Not held or Obtainable, and anything held can be worn, taken off, shown or hidden from the page. It is readable on every theme and usable with a keyboard or a gamepad, and the Codex is translated. Each armor piece, shield and weapon held adds its appearance to the collection, listed under a new Appearances part, and Trophies, Fish and Annals parts were added. An Aura Armory part shows all pieces, the ones held and how to get each.
Cosmetic Shop ||| A Shop on the pause screen, opened with the gem to the right of the laurel, sells cosmetic items that give no power, with all proceeds going to the costs of developing DFO Online. It shows its items in three tabs, Titles, Glyphs and Auras, each with its look, price and status. Buying needs an account with a username and is paid in US dollars through Stripe in the browser, so the game does not get card details, and the item goes to the account with a receipt in the mailbox, to be worn from the profile or Collections page. The first items, sold only during the pre-alpha and kept after the sale ends, are Genesis, Origin and Eternal. The shop shows the price in the player's own currency when Stripe can, and offers more ways to pay such as iDEAL according to the country, while its prices are in US dollars and a credit card is not necessary. It also has an Aura Armory section: the Eternal pieces cost $6 each and The Eternal Set gives all five for $20, the shop showing each piece and the set before buying.
Courtship and Marriage ||| Courtship and marriage belong to Project Legacy. A townsperson who counts the player a friend can be courted by asking about Courtship in conversation, once a day, depending on Personality, Etiquette and tone, and when their heart is full the player can ask for their hand and is wed at the temple of their town. The spouse joins the house, keeping their own face and day, and every thirty days there is a one-in-four chance of a child, at most six with each spouse, who cannot be played until the mantle passes to them. If someone being courted dies the courtship ends. Online, two players can wed by standing in a temple together and pressing Propose marriage on the other, each keeping their own house, and word of a spouse's death or deletion reaches the player the next time they are online. Courtship counts a tone as it was taken: a tone the person likes helps the day's courtship and one they dislike costs a little affection (never below nothing), and they say so. A spouse has the same race in the world and on the Family tab.
Crew ||| The crew are the named hands on the player's ship. Each near crew member is a real animated character with a green health bar, a name and a job such as First Mate, Bard, Bosun, Gunner or Carpenter, remembers fights and boardings, and chats in pairs. The crew strike up a sea shanty led by a bard, hurry about when the guns are out, and line the rail when grapples are thrown. The crew lasts twice as long as before, since cannon fire, fires on deck, rams and the sea serpent's blows take half as many men, though swivel-gun boats still go for the crew first at half the old pace and enemy crews are unchanged. Hands lost in a boarding cost the ship that much crew, the player's own blows and spells pass their crew by, and the player cannot pickpocket their own crew or a town's defenders. A ship's crew keeps its race instead of changing with the player's location.
Death Penalty ||| The death penalty is the gold lost when dying. It was a quarter of the gold in the purse, and since 0.1.6068 it is a tenth online. Three coins or fewer lose nothing, bank gold and letters of credit are safe, the death screen says exactly how much before the player rises, and a resurrection by a party member costs nothing. Offline, when Project Legacy raises the player after a death they now also lose a tenth of the gold they carry. In the Cyrodiil zone the remains stay in the world where the player fell, and a killer's laid remains show the player's name instead of "Traveller", appearing as the player's own remains on their screen and the map but not on the killer's. If the game had lost its link to the social service a death took nothing from the pack while the killer still took the items into the remains; now the pack loses the items that go into the remains.
Desktop App ||| The desktop app is the downloadable version of the game, with saves as real files, built on Electron 42. The Windows installer and the Linux AppImage update themselves, the portable exe and the Mac tell the player when a new version is out with a Download button, the app has its own icon, and it finds and installs updates again. It is not yet code-signed. Updating no longer leaves the game missing or unpacks 18,000 files every launch. Downloads are named DFO-Online-... from the rename on, and an installed copy finds its update as usual. On a Mac the new version installs as DFO Online beside the old Daggerfall Online app (to be dragged to the Bin once open), and on Windows pinned taskbar or Start menu entries may need repinning under the new name. It keeps the account and settings files in memory instead of reading them from disk every frame, as the browser does, giving smoother frames in a town. The app updates the game one file at a time instead of downloading about 130 MB and replacing the whole game folder, the full installer running only when the app itself changes or a file-by-file update fails, on Windows, macOS, Linux and the portable build. The Windows desktop app keeps loading the world when its window is minimized or covered by another window, and keeps the compiled game scripts between launches as a web browser does.
Enemy Sync ||| Enemy Sync keeps other players' enemies the same for everyone online. In busy areas enemies no longer die for one player and live on for another, or vanish when their controller leaves, dies or goes through a door, and at a crowded Starfall every player's enemies reach everyone in turn. Enemies run by a lagging player move smoothly for others, and on a slow computer enemies and the town watch keep pace with the player instead of moving at half speed. Online, all players in a game meet the same creatures, on the Bay and past it. In multiplayer all players see the same race for one enemy.
Enhanced AI ||| Enhanced AI changes how foes fight. A foe waiting its turn turns and fights when approached instead of backing away, the step back after a blow is under half a second, and hurt foes back off at most once every 10 seconds. An archer waiting its turn stands still facing the player, retreats once for up to 2 seconds when closed on, then fights hand to hand and goes back to shooting once the player is about 6 m away. It is on by default offline as well as online, and can be switched back to the classic enemy AI in Settings > Combat > Enemies, though pressing All off in the features list keeps it off. Foes use a map of the trees, crates and other objects they hide behind that takes about a third of its old memory, and find their line of sight faster; the objects that stop sight and missiles do not change.
Graphics Backend ||| The Windows desktop app has a Graphics backend setting in Settings > Graphics > Display, Direct3D 11 (the default) or Vulkan, taking effect the next time the app starts. On many graphics cards Vulkan prepares the game's graphics much faster, so the first launch after an update does not freeze for long, though play is a little slower than on Direct3D 11. Wait For Screen Refresh stays on with Vulkan, since the game froze on resizing the window when it was off, and if Vulkan cannot start the app says so and starts again on Direct3D 11, trying Vulkan again after a graphics driver update or an app update with a new graphics engine. On Vulkan the windows do not blur the world behind them (it drew upside down behind the Inventory) and keep their dark color, while Direct3D 11 and the browser keep the blur.
Harbours ||| Port towns feature moored ships with stowed sails that arrive and depart, merchantmen, patrolling navy ships, and offshore pirates. Coastal seats can raise a Harbour, berths and quays lie further from shore where the sea floor shelves gently, harbours far from streets don't constantly search for docks, lake confusion is fixed, ports/lighthouses/quays run lighter, and docks no longer cross each other. Seven more towns on the coast past the Bay have a harbour and a shipyard: Farrun, Shornhelm, Blacklight, Tear, Thorn, Elden Root and Silinorin, with ships sailing between these ports and the ports near them.
Hearth Offerings ||| A Hearth Offering is set out at the door of a home during the Witches' Festival and filled with Soul Cakes. Other players can take one cake from each offering per day, and the owner can take it back in at any time, even after the festival, with the bowl and every cake still in it. When other players have taken 25 cakes from a player's offerings the player earns the Hearthkeeper title, and taking 10 cakes from others' offerings earns the Soul Cake icon. Interacting in a town with an offering no longer crashes the game, and the names under the crosshair show again. The Hearth Offering at a door is a carved pumpkin on a stool below a lantern.
Held Enchantments ||| An item with the Cast When Held enchantment loses 1 point of condition every 3 minutes, where it used to lose 3 points each minute.
Home Yards ||| Every home can put 60 decorations in its yard, also in a busy town, as a town's yards no longer fill up. Decorations near the player appear first and the game fetches only changes near the player rather than the full town each minute, and asks less often when nothing changes nearby; a yard change shows to other players near it in a few seconds, and decorations in other players' yards are drawn in groups for a higher frame rate in large cities. An older version of the game shows no yard decorations of other players, and a reload or restarting the desktop app updates it.
Homes ||| Homes have a Who may enter setting, including My guild. Room furniture can be taken out, knightly order free houses are never owned by other players, graveyard crypts are not sold as homes online, and online homes/guild halls on the arena's block move to new houses on login without emptying storage. A phantom room tab in two-storey homes (often "Room 3") has been fixed. A home's chests no longer lose their contents when furniture fails to load, anything without a chest or box to go in being set down at the player's feet. When the servers are under heavy load the game waits longer between tries to load a town's homes, and loading a town's homes costs the servers half as many database requests. A home's furnishings load faster, especially when the server is busy. A home's crafting stations (forge, workbench, loom, mason's and jeweller's benches, alchemy and enchanting) can be used by the account's other characters as well as the buyer, while the storage still belongs to the character who bought it. A house can be bought in all of Cyrodiil, the open country too, and in a town on an isle of the Eltheric Ocean.
Immersive Travel ||| Immersive Travel (by kkgobkk) places a carriage, horses and a driver outside the gates of walled cities. The player picks a town or village and pays a small daily fare to fast travel there, and carriages go to cities, hamlets and villages only, never putting the player on a ship. Disable Normal Travel is off by default and, when on, makes carriages the only fast travel, taking effect at the next start. Online a carriage is the way to fast travel over land, with the same carriages, fares and rules for everyone, and vampires and sunlight-hurt characters cannot take one by day. Carriage drivers take the player to towns in other provinces under the same rules and fare as in the Iliac Bay.
Keybindings ||| The keybinding standard (KB1) was introduced and audited, and shared keys were reworked in 0.1.4122. A key that is set but not confirmed says "Not saved yet - press Confirm." on its row, the top of the page counts the unsaved changes and highlights Confirm, and the Confirm bar no longer hides behind the Settings search bar or covers the row being bound on a phone. Polling a bound key no longer builds a list for every binding. Swing Weapon bound to Left Click, with mouse activate unbound, survives a restart. When a key is assigned that is already in use, the confirmation appears beside the binding being changed instead of at the top of the controls list. A tutorial page or message opening over a window (spellbook, inventory, chronicle, shop windows, tavern, player trade, pause menu, character sheet or level-up screen) gets Enter and Escape, so Escape no longer closes the window under it and Enter no longer pushes its button, such as confirming points on the level-up screen.
Launch Speed ||| The first launch after an update no longer freezes for half a minute while the game prepares its graphics, as the work runs in the background while the world loads, and the first seconds of play no longer stop while effects such as weapon glows and foe warnings get ready. On a slower or busy computer the volumetric clouds no longer stop the first launch, the sky showing simpler clouds until the full ones are ready, and the pictures of the character's body load in the background. The game keeps some results of the first launch (town ground, ship models and pictures, shipyard and lighthouse sites, and the map of Tamriel), using about 9 MB of storage, which makes the first launch a little slower and later launches' pauses in Daggerfall shorter. After logging in, the game prepares effect shaders one at a time instead of all at once, which removed a freeze of 10 to 15 seconds on some computers, though other players' auras can appear some seconds after the players.
Light Spell ||| In first person the candle of the Light spell is not shown, though the light stays; other players and the third-person view still show the candle.
Market ||| The Market is a Notice Board tab for trading in Marks or gold, featuring grouped views (Buy, Sell, History), silver display, column names, supplier counters, and detailed split lists. Listings stand 72 hours for a 1% fee with immediate sales (minus 5% tax) and courier delivery, with strict gold/Drakes currency separation and item exclusions. Seat boards, market sales, and courier deliveries no longer slow down account services in regions where a guild holds a Charter. Market stalls look the way the pack draws them, with the keeper behind the counter and goods on the counter. Selecting an item in Crafted, Goods or Auctions shows its card with the picture, tier, stats and magic, and the item bought is the same as the one on the card.
Morrowind Body ||| The Morrowind body features climbing poses (hanging, shimmying, wall climbing, leaps, pull-overs, vault tucks), gripping animations, first-person hand reach, crowd optimization, port-made steel plate armor sets, synchronized attack speeds, spell-casting animations, and T-pose fixes. Placed home/yard decoration NPCs stand in Morrowind bodies, you can ride in third person, look down over the 3D horse's neck in first person, and 3D horses match sprite coats with unique flank markings. With the Morrowind model in first person the arms grip the wall while climbing even with the weapon sheathed. The cape hangs from the chest alone and moves only with it as one stiff sheet, so walking, swinging the arms and sitting no longer bend it, and leaning back swings the whole cape with the chest. Looting a fallen enemy in a dungeon no longer turns its Morrowind body back into the old sprite while the loot window is open, bodies no longer clip through floors and walls, and chain boots, gauntlets and pauldrons, including Legendary ones like The Wayrest Courier's Treads, show on the body (in iron) instead of bare skin. When paused in a Morrowind body the player can select Morrowind or Sprite to change the body shown.
Motherlodes ||| Motherlodes are daily rich ore heaps announced in chat and marked on the compass, requiring Mining 25 and Pick-Axe strikes for ore, XP, and silver without counting against harvest limits, and neither motherlodes nor veins, boulders, herbs, and trees appear inside rocks. Motherlodes appear on the compass from their warning onward, in their own colour, drawn over nearby veins. With Motherlode Sense (Mining 100) the day's Motherlodes are marked on the map and the Overworld from the start of the day, each showing its ore and, until it rises, when it rises (such as "Tue 13:00", on the player's own clock), with warnings still coming 30 minutes ahead. Motherlodes past the Veil still rise only in the Iliac Bay. After midnight UTC the Motherlodes can show some minutes late.
Online Saving ||| Online saves can be up to 16 MB, up from 4, and are compressed before going to the realm. An outage no longer costs online progress because a kept copy of the save survives closing the page, and closing the game no longer loses the last minutes. A kept save is used only if nothing newer reached the server from another device, trade or purchase, and an older game copy can read the character but cannot save one grown this big. An online character saves itself every five minutes instead of two, while trades, crafting, profession work and anything the server settles still save at once. An online close or reload keeps its last save, which the player gets back on joining again, instead of losing progress since the last automatic save. If the realm does not get a session's guild reputation the next device keeps it and the game says so, the game says when the realm takes no save for 5 minutes or cannot save (during a duel, for instance), and it saves at once when another device takes the online seat. Progress that a device kept but the realm did not get can be recovered from the character's tile at the Online door as a new offline character, and changing devices with the app open no longer sets a save aside.
Overworld ||| The Overworld is a travel view over the real world, where the player clicks to travel and sees other players, weather from above, the sea, shared roaming bands, camps and dungeons. The controls are one compact block in the bottom-right corner, with a Path switch that is remembered: Roads (default) prefers road travel and Free walks across country around mountains and across water. A Show section filters Towns, Distant, Dungeons, Enemies, Travellers and Gathering groups, and since 0.1.6646 these filters sit in their own box at the bottom left, which folds away and can be moved like the rest of the HUD. A Nodes button picks how far gathering groups show, and right-clicking the map places a waypoint (see Waypoints). Double-clicking a band or camp marker asks "Attack the Zombie band?", and Yes travels straight toward them at full speed. Players close together show as one larger dot, only the six nearest show full title, Renown, tag and glyphs, towns with a carriage driver show a wheel, and friends' names are blue and guild-mates' violet, with player filters on both maps. Hovering a capturable town shows who holds it and this week's battle. Routes go around World of Daggerfall mountains, and rain, snow, sand and wind fill the air in front of the camera. The dungeons near the player (Daggerfall's own, random dungeons online and a standing Abyss Dungeon) show their own entrance model, enlarged so they can be seen from above, and the Overworld no longer refuses a walk over mountains. At high travel speeds (x100) the character, the ring under them and the route line move smoothly, the speed no longer drops to x60 for the rest of a trip after a small gap in the road, and the ring under the player is smaller, centred on their feet and rimmed dark. The route is a trail of gold dots fixed to the ground, the clicked spot is a gold X (a slightly larger one for a town), and map dots and diamonds are symbols in the same colours: a skull for roaming bands, a stone arch for dungeons (red if undiscovered, brass if found), a person for party members and other players (two for a crowd), a backpack for pilgrims and pedlars, and a pickaxe, pine, leaf, paw print and fish for Mining, Logging, Herbalism, Hunting and Fishing nodes. Ships, their map lookups and wagon harness ropes do the same work for less effort and memory garbage, so walking the coast runs faster. A found dungeon shows as a dungeon on the Overworld grid. Overworld travel no longer drops to 1x speed and stays there, and land that fails to load is loaded again instead of leaving a hole. Travelling fast no longer spends time building ground already passed, and the land ahead loads up to three times faster, so holes in the view and drops to 1x speed are much rarer, including on straight roads. While ground is still loading the view may run at about 30 fps instead of 60 until the land is built. The game finds a route for more long journeys past the Iliac Bay, so a journey from Daggerfall to the Imperial City no longer stops with "There is no way there by land", and no longer stops for many seconds when a very long journey begins.
Passage Under Sail ||| Friendly merchant and navy ships moored at a port's quays can carry the player: look at her hull and press Activate, and her captain lists every port the player knows, nearest first, with the direction it lies and the fare. After confirming, the player boards at her helm and the voyage is sailed across the Overworld, with nothing paid until she sets sail. If the voyage stops she sails on by herself, at the far port she lands the player, and stepping off her anywhere ends the passage, anything put in her hold coming back to the pack. She is never the player's own ship. Online, any player at the port can book a ship, other players can board and ride along, and once she has sailed she is gone from that berth for the rest of the day. "Under Sail" counts a voyage in any boat, counted from where the player last lay still, and running aground in the next harbour or leaving the helm as the ship comes in counts as making port. A voyage survives a reload or a crash between the two ports, Pencart's harbour can be reached, and the harbourmaster says that a port counts when it is another port's harbour a quarter league off or more. Ships sail from Daggerfall to each port past the Bay and back, round the provinces between, and a passage to a port outside the Bay continues after a save is loaded; ports found before stay on the ship captains' list. The commission of a province is also the Emperor's writ of passage, so a friendly ship's master sails the player to Daggerfall free of charge until arrival there. Travel by ship from one port to another past the Bay no longer stops at the coast, the ship keeping to the water near a coast and in a narrow channel such as the Niben.
Province Peoples ||| Each province past the Iliac Bay has its own people, most persons in Skyrim being Nords, in Morrowind Dark Elves and in Cyrodiil Imperials, with some persons from neighbours (Imperial soldiers and traders in Morrowind, Khajiit in Valenwood) and some areas of a different people (Reachmen in the Reach, many Khajiit in Reaper's March, many Imperials in Gideon, many Argonians and Khajiit in Leyawiin). Persons in the street, residents, keepers, port crews, guards and enemies all follow these rules and take a name from their own people. Inside buildings more persons now show a body of the correct race, in the Bay too, and some persons past the Bay have a new name and race after the update, though a courtship already started keeps its name and race.
PvP Health ||| In the open zone in Cyrodiil, in a duel and in a siege, a hit from a player removes the player's own health, shown on the health bar, as a part of health, so a strong and a weak character lose the same part from the same hit. For 30 seconds after a player hit, and throughout a duel or siege, the player cannot heal, and health from a potion or spell in that time does not stay; health a player removes does not return by itself after 30 seconds. A lost duel ends with 1 health and does not heal, and in a siege the player returns with full health at their wave.
Revenants ||| Revenants are special foes that kill the player or escape them and return to hunt. Elite and Champion foes of level 3 or more that kill the player become their revenant, as do special foes that flee below 20% health and anything that finishes the player with a spell or poison. They rank up each kill or escape to rank 5, each rank adding +25% health and +10% damage, and up to 5 can hunt at once. Each has a personality (Brutal, Witty, Humorous, Arrogant, Cold, Zealous, Unhinged, Honourable, Craven or Weary) and a named epithet, and learns from how it was beaten, returning with up to three adaptations, never immunity, and a hidden weakness that takes 50% more once revealed. From rank 3 its will must be broken before it kneels. A beaten revenant kneels and the player chooses Kill or Spare in the loot window: Kill is an execution that drops a unique Magic, Rare or Legendary weapon of its own plus all it carried, and Spare swears it as a companion for good, while leaving it 90 seconds makes it escape and rank up. Each has a lair, kin and a signature attack, and one that kills the player online takes an unlocked item. Under Project Legacy the revenant that killed an ancestor comes after their heir. Online, revenants wait for Renown 10: below it no foe becomes a revenant or breaks and runs to become one and none comes back, while existing revenants are held and marked Held on the Revenants page with when they will come. Revenants come back a little less often everywhere, a revenant that is due now taking 2 in 5 encounter chances instead of 1 in 2. Another player's Disintegrate no longer deletes the player's revenant outright but counts as a killing blow, and only the owner decides their revenant's fate. Not every elite or champion can become a revenant: about one in four is marked Unquiet ("Unquiet Elite Orc") and only a marked foe can rise, at most three hunt the player at once, and one comes back after two to four days. A marked foe breaks and runs at a third of its health one time in two, so most revenants are born by getting away, and a revenant ranks up every time it gets away (killing the player or tearing away from the killing blow ranks it only every second time, and from rank II its will must be broken or it tears away). When one gets away in the open it goes to ground in the nearest dungeon and leaves a trail, weakened at 60% of its health if reached within twelve hours, and left alone its wounds heal, the oldest every five days. From rank II each flees its own way (Smoke for people, Blink for casters, spirits, daedra and atronachs, Sink for skeletons, zombies, mummies, vampires and spriggans, Bolt for beasts and monsters), the blow that broke it leaves a wound (a blade bleeds it, a hammer or fist cracks it, an axe maims it, an arrow lames it, an element sears it, three kept), and it wants Vengeance, Mastery or Plunder by temperament, with omens on the day it returns. Each rank adds +40% health, +15% damage and +3 levels for a class foe (sworn revenants keep the old values), and a rank-V revenant that gets away once more becomes the Dread, which goes to ground for good, never comes to the player, has half again its health, makes two last stands and drops a Legendary trophy. The player's own Disintegrate no longer deletes a revenant: it still breaks through the last stand and will but brings the revenant to its knees like any killing blow, to Kill (its trophy weapon drops) or Spare (it joins as a companion). An enemy that kills the player and comes back as a revenant keeps its race.
Shared Containers ||| Shared Containers covers a building's cupboards and chests and the coffins and other search spots in dungeons, each one shared list for everyone using it. Closing a window no longer restores an item someone took or erases one someone put in, two players opening a cupboard see the same contents, and Take all and Stash all land every piece. A find from a coffin or search spot is shared by the party as one list with one copy of each piece, two separate groups searching the same coffin each loot their own find, and searching a spot again after its five hours locks it for the whole party again. An item put into a cupboard the building's host never accepted comes back on leaving, and players on an older game version still see the old behaviour in the same room until they reload. Items that went from a chest in an online home on October 25, after a shared chest was made private again, come back into it the next time the player goes into the home, with a message saying how many came back, and a chest shared and then made private again no longer loses items that went from it before.
Ships at Sea ||| Pirates, merchantmen and the navies of Daggerfall, Wayrest and Sentinel sail the Iliac Bay, each with a name and a captain and sailing the same boats the player can own. Pirates attack anything, merchantmen run, and a navy hunts pirates and the player once notorious enough, with most pirates only coming for a ship they outgun or one crippled and running from one that outguns them. Ships sail by the wind, keep off the rocks and fight broadside to broadside, a hostile ship nearby counts as an enemy nearby blocking fast travel and rest, and ships fight each other on their own, with the navy hearing cannon fire and sailing toward it. When a ship strikes to another the winner boards her and sets her on fire, a ship with no crew left surrenders, a galley's ram forces surrender rather than sinking, and packets sail lanes between neighbouring port towns on one shared schedule, berthing, waiting and sailing on. Friendly ships have green health bars and turn hostile if fired on, ships are named for their trade and waters, and the Ships at sea setting decides how many are out, none when it is off. Warm Ashes' Overworld raiders are real ships at sea. Sea ships fly their own colours: navies use their kingdom's paint and royal ensign (Daggerfall's blue with the dagger, Wayrest's crimson with the rose, Sentinel's gold with the sun), merchant ships belong to one of eight trading houses with the house's paint and mark (the Isles' teal past the Veil), pirates use one of six crews' dark paint and black flags with false colours painted as the merchant house until the black flag is raised, and legendary ships have their own colours. A ship that surrenders, sinks or is captured lowers its flag, and cloth flags show on ships within 450 m (up to 24 at once) with the old pennant further away. Ships past the Iliac Bay sail into their port instead of turning in circles near the coast.
Snowfall ||| In winter, outside the deserts, real snow lies on the ground, deeper in the wild than in towns, building up while snow falls and melting back in clear weather. The player, monsters, guards and townsfolk leave tracks in it, which slowly fill back in (faster while it snows) and are kept in the save, and a fallen enemy leaves a hollow where it lay. With Basic Roads, paths and tracks keep only a thin layer of snow and main roads get a bank along their edges, and the winter ground under it has new textures. It has its own switch in Features, on by default in the enhanced outdoors. Foes' ground telegraphs and the player's technique marks always draw on top of deep snow, and deep snow can be turned off at Settings > Graphics > Sky, water & weather > Snowfall. The snow around the player is prepared in the background, so the frame rate is more stable when riding or flying fast over winter ground.
Soul Cake ||| A Soul Cake has no weight and does not add to encumbrance.
Staff Events ||| Staff events are a staff tool for running a monster event on a city or area, with a hidden kill meter and a yellow mini boss. The mini boss always spawns and a boss appears each time the meter fills, every player sees the mini boss's real health, and it stands at the biggest group. Packs keep coming while the boss waits, more and faster with more players, and the reward is earned by kills alone. Staff can put a town event in a town outside the Iliac Bay and an area event past it, the list of places showing the provinces. Play as boss was fixed, with one boss, its health bar, spell damage numbers, the Dwarven Dragon monster and its Admin ride, and Casual characters online.
Teleporters ||| Red brick teleporters that the player walks into now always send them on; before, some only worked when clicked. A guild's teleport map lists only the cities past the Bay as destinations. The Mages Guild teleport, Recall and the Portal Stone take the player to and from a city, town or village in Cyrodiil, the Imperial City among them, and a city is selected by pushing it on the teleport map, but not to or from other areas of the open PvP zone, which are reached on its roads. A Portal Stone can open a portal to any land past the Bay but online not into Cyrodiil. The Mages Guild teleport also sends the player to known places outside the Iliac Bay, such as farms and temples.
Town Layouts ||| Town layouts are the town mods' redrawn towns. Houses, rooms, quest buildings, smith items, Recall anchors and indoor saves keep their layout, and if a town cannot be shown in the house's layout the house is not treated as the player's there. Online, a home cannot be bought until the game has loaded the towns as other players see them, players who see a town differently are never put in the same building, both mods are set for the whole room and every home keeps its town's layout from purchase. Fixes covered building colours for climate, saves made inside new-town buildings, windmills on the new farms, the Order of the Raven's halls, the castles of Daggerfall, Sentinel and Wayrest, temple doors blocked by foundations, quest people appearing under floors or in attics, 34 Arkay temples left with no houses, and crop fields growing through walls. Town ponds no longer show flat painted squares, six lone island tiles are dry ground, and some pond edges the puddle removal had wrongly cleared are back. Every player in a room stands on the same ground, even if a town pack failed to load for one of them. The ground at the edge of a town in the Iliac Bay has no high cliffs, holes or pits, fewer steep slopes and no pit next to a high hill, a town near the sea goes down to the water on a slope with piers and docks meeting the shore, and crops stand on the ground. City walls do not flicker near their towers and gates (Riften's included), have no doors, do not go through each other at turns, and each road to a walled city in the Bay goes to a gate. Stables, wagon yards and joineries mostly stay in their first location, moving only if a road or wall is on them; a camp, anchor or dropped item near a town stays on the new ground.
Travel Map ||| The travel map is a parchment held in the player's hands, drawn as a held sprite with a Features switch, a Morrowind map on every sheet, readable glyphs and a fourth painting in the hands. A default trip is walked, the map was audited several times, and players riding another's boat are marked on the map where their boat is. The Overworld travel view no longer leaves 1 to 1.7 GB of extra memory behind after a journey, and it draws faster while a route is on screen. Clicking near a town walks around it and clicking its middle goes in, and with "travel only to places" on, clicking anywhere on a town travels to it. A place past the Iliac Bay can be clicked on the travel map to open the travel panel, and Begin journey walks there on the overworld (a walk, not fast travel), also to open land when Travel Options lets the player travel to a spot, but a click on the sea starts no journey. The character goes to the gate of a city, dungeon or quest location rather than the center of its map square, and to cities on the coast such as Solitude, which the game used to stop with a message about the water. A waypoint on land past the Bay shows Travel here, one on the water does not, and the travel map now takes the player to cities, towns, villages and other places in all provinces as in the Bay.
Travel Pathing ||| Travel pathing was reworked in 0.1.4216. Planned routes stay on the road from start to finish, including around corners, through towns on diagonal roads and up to a spot clicked near a road. Road travel keeps to the roads, leaving them only near the start and end of a journey or where no road joins two places, and a party walks the road as drawn, round each bend. On curved roads past the Bay the party keeps its speed and a steady direction instead of stopping at each bend.
Weapon Swap ||| Outside the open PvP zone a weapon change takes half the time; in Cyrodiil, on the open sea and in their dungeons it does not change.
