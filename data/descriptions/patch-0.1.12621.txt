Abyss Dungeons ||| An Abyss Dungeon is the hardest dungeon in the game, online only. One at a time rises beside a busy city in the region with the most players online, with a column of brass-gold light over it, and the first player to reach its door finds it and everyone online hears where, after which its ring, compass mark, Timers row and notice-board note stay until it is gone. It has three foes at every spot, each four times as tough and hitting two and a half times as hard, six elite foes, half again the loot and no campfires. At its deepest point stands the Rift into the Shattered Hour, where the Orrery of Endings, the Unmoored Steps and the Brass Remnant are met, and the Remnant's fight, spoils, collapse and one-life rule are shared by everyone in the Hour at once. Each Abyss Dungeon keeps one of six Endings and two of nine omens, and fighters earn Hours broken toward the Hourbreaker title and The Turning Hour aura. Crew and sworn companions wait outside the Rift, and leaving the Hour for any reason puts the player on the ground at the dungeon's door. The Rift takes a party to its own Shattered Hour, which other parties cannot enter, while the Hollow before it stays shared; a player outside a party goes to a shared Hour of 8 players, and the next player to a new one when it is full or its boss is dead. A message says whose Hour it is, or how many are in a shared one. A player who dies can go back into the same Hour but not a different one in that Hollow, and when a party kills the boss only its own Hour closes.
Alinor ||| Alinor's Crystal Tower is no longer in the harbour; the Royal Palace stands on a terraced height at the top of a grand stair, and a river goes through the south of the city to the sea under two white bridges.
Ashfall ||| Ashfall is a weather of the volcanic lands of Morrowind, falling about half the time on Red Mountain, a third in the Ashlands and less in Stonefalls. Gray ash falls slowly under a dark brown sky with small embers that glow red and orange and go out, and from a distance a dark column of ash shows below an ash cloud. The travel forecast and the survival temperature tell when it will fall, and it is part of Features > Weather as places, on by default.
Auras ||| Auras are cosmetic effects worn from the aura picker on the account card, each granted with a title. They include the Oblivion Ward, Golden Radiance, the Holo Shadow Cloak (with the Shadow Fang title), the developers' Seraph Wings and Crystal Resonance (with the Crystal Fist title). An aura now shows to everyone nearby within a second or two, up to 32 are drawn at once, and the cloak and wings fold away while riding or swimming. They sit correctly on both Morrowind bodies and Eye of the Beholder sprites. Lilac Warren is SylviaBun's aura: six lilac bunnies with cotton tails and little maid's headdresses hop round the player's feet on a ring of lilac light, leaving fading paw-print tracks, with an occasional happy binky and sparkles of lilac, pink and white. The Elite Breach adds Beaconlight (Redoubt 10), The Burning Stair (Redoubt 20) and Firstlight. Eternal is a $20 aura, a cape of blue and violet fire on the character's back that moves when the character walks; it hangs behind the body, as the Seraph Wings do, and no longer in front when seen from the side.
Bounty Boards ||| Bounty boards are notice boards in towns with two or more notice boards, where half become Bounty Boards. Every board in a town posts the same four hunts to every player, and notices change each in-game day. Pressing one opens the board window in the Enhanced Plus style. Clicking Take bounty repeatedly takes the board's notices in order from the top, after the last take the notice shows a short line instead of buttons, Give up asks for a second click and sits on the left, and the board keeps one size so the Take button stays put. A board gives hunts for the monsters living near its town, on the Bay and across Tamriel, with their names in all the languages of the game.
Bounty Rewards ||| Killing the last monster completes the bounty, and a reward notice pauses the game, tells a short story and lists exactly what was received. A full pack of 8 pays the tier's full purse and each monster fewer takes 10% off, from 30 to 50 gold at tier 1 up to about 150 to 200 at level 15 and above. The item reward is one random weapon or armour piece, never higher than blue, and its condition and chance of being Magic rise with the tier. A Bounty at a seat past the Bay pays when the player clears an outlaw hideout or a lair near the town.
Bridges ||| Rivers wider than a ford have bridges, stone for roads and timber for tracks, that can be walked and ridden across and are shown on the map; the Niben below Lake Rumare is a sea, crossed by ship.
Capitals ||| The game builds seven capitals by hand: the Imperial City, Solitude, Mournhold, Alinor, Falinesti, Torval and Helstrom, each following the lore and the games more closely and with new stone, wood, slate and gilt textures that match Daggerfall's resolution. Each has a door into the halls of its castle (on the White-Gold Tower in the Imperial City), no road crosses its walls, lake or harbour, and its roads stop at its gates, with a road to each capital. Alinor and Torval stand a step above the water of their harbours.
Caravans ||| A merchant's caravan travels with a horse and wagon (two wagons from a great house's city) with its guards walking in front and behind, a noble's procession brings a baggage wagon, and pedlars and farmers lead a pack horse. The wagons' wheels turn, at camp the wagons are parked round one fire without standing in each other with each horse tied alongside its cart, and a wagon follows its horse round a bend. Caravans and travellers keep to the right of the road, and a caravan's mark on the travel map is a wagon. Talking to a caravan's merchant, a farmer or a pedlar allows buying and selling from a shop's stock and a limited purse, with friends getting 10% off. A merchant's caravan offers a guard contract from the day before it sets out until it arrives, paid by the day plus more for each foe beaten if the player stays within 300 m, and unpaid if they fall behind over an hour, let the merchant die or rob or kill any of them; offline the player can travel on with it to its next stop. Beating every guard of a caravan makes it surrender and its goods and purse are the player's to take, but any survivor who reaches a town reports the theft, pickpocketing or murder as a crime in the region where it happened. A road caravan's campfire is a fire again with Morrowind models on, and a roadside fight the player walks up to no longer ends by itself. If the overhead lantern or another built-in piece is taken out of a caravan, visitors no longer see it or its light. Caravans outside the Iliac Bay meet the animals and monsters of their own province.
Character Creation ||| Character creation is the screen where a new character is made. The creator tells the player what they rolled, a phone version exists, and a character made online no longer gets a skill check the moment it is created. The Project Legacy question (Enduring, Bloodline, No lineage) and the leveling question are Enhanced Plus popups in which the option under the pointer is the one highlighted and clicking it selects it. Additionally, heads and hairs are now paired freely, from every pairing of the heads and hairs your Morrowind data offers a race at character creation, whereas there were previously ten. Classes are grouped as Magic, Stealth and Combat and each shows the two attributes it relies on. The class card shows the class's attributes, skills and health per level before its description, with health reading as the real range (for example "3-6 a level, more with Endurance"). On phones "Begin your life" and "Play as..." stay on screen, the province names on the map no longer overlap, the drums play only when a class is chosen, Start over sits beside Back, and buttons say "Play as an Acrobat". Small text is at least 12px. Online character creation has no biography questions. Its last page asks where the story begins, in the Iliac Bay or in a province past it, and on a phone a start is tapped to see its story and tapped again, or Begin here, to select it.
Coasts ||| Walking or sailing along a coastline no longer stalls the game while the surf is rebuilt, and the far coasts were fixed. Towns on the coast stand on their own land, not dragged down toward the sea. Coastal towns no longer stand behind cliffs at the beach line; the strand rises to them gently. Water, sand, grass and stone meet on soft, uneven lines, and where the deep sea is cut out the shore ends on an uneven line, not on square steps.
Cosmetic Shop ||| A Shop on the pause screen, opened with the gem to the right of the laurel, sells cosmetic items that give no power, with all proceeds going to the costs of developing DFO Online. It shows its items in three tabs, Titles, Glyphs and Auras, each with its look, price and status. Buying needs an account with a username and is paid in US dollars through Stripe in the browser, so the game does not get card details, and the item goes to the account with a receipt in the mailbox, to be worn from the profile or Collections page. The first items, sold only during the pre-alpha and kept after the sale ends, are Genesis, Origin and Eternal.
Crown Castle ||| A crown-holder's castle throne room is its hall, with the guild's banners behind the throne. The Guildmaster of the guild holding a crown region can buy its castle for 120,000 silver as an instance of the guild alone, getting half back if the region is lost or 85% if the castle is given up. The castles are the three crown castles of the Bay and those of six capitals past it, and the halls of the Imperial City are open to all.
Crowns ||| A crown's holder earns a quarter more influence at its kingdom's palace seats, Tribute excepted. Seven provinces past the Bay have a crown seat at their capital, each city-state of Arena a palace seat, and the seven are kingdoms with their own crown, colors and banner, their crown sieges on Friday one hour apart; each province outside the Bay has its own ruler, court and people.
Distant Land ||| Distant land is the far scenery. It has live-distance fog, a far-ring level of detail with skirts, and a far province ring with real mountains on the horizon. The far view no longer shows trees or shapes above the horizon. With the enhanced graphics mountain ranges show up to 300 km away and far land shows woods in the colors of their trees, hills and trees do not appear suddenly as the land loads, trees at a distance stay after a long journey, and forests and ground past the Bay show each region's trees and colors.
Docked Ships ||| Docked ships keep their berths. A ship left made fast at a quay keeps her berth out of sight so none moors inside her, summon and docking agree, and online another player's ship keeps a berth even while her captain is invisible at her helm. Moored boats no longer play their sounds at full volume after the player leaves a building or a caravan. Moored ships no longer stand inside a town's walls or on its piers, Solitude's ships lie at its docks outside the walls, and every port past the Bay, Lillandril included, has its moored ships.
Elite Gates ||| The Elite Breach is the tier above Dagon's Breach: a party of up to 8 goes down through the torn halls of the Battlespire under a 30-minute count, opened with a Weir Key used outdoors, online and signed in (or the free Rehearsal, the first circle of five halls, paying nothing), the party at that moment becoming the company and being called. A start window chooses Hall 1 or a reached Redoubt, a Wager of up to three decrees can be taken on, and an arch with a 400 m gold beacon rises where the key is used. There are four regions, the Broken Galleries (circles 1-5), the Soul Cairn (6-10), the Shade Perilous (11-15) and the Havoc Wellhead (16 and deeper), and each circle is five halls (a fight, a crossing, a trial, a second fight and the lord's Court) with Yards, Ramparts, crossings of drifting, blinking and crumbling platforms, Rails, Gusts, Pendulums and Vents, trials, twists, sixteen kinds of enemy and thirteen lords, a Valkynaz every fifth Court. Every enemy blow is drawn on the floor before it lands, the Count runs during fights, falling into the void casts the player back for a tenth of their health, a death is a fall after which the next Redoubt calls the player back once and a witness can ping the floor, and the Spire has no saving, resting, map, Mark, Recall, levitation or companions. A Redoubt (taken by felling a circle's lord) records itself on the account, heals the company and calls the fallen back, offers three Vestiges, votes on the next circle's decrees and pays once per arena week with gold, a Rare or Legendary piece, more Magic-or-better pieces, the lord's named Legendary 1 in 8, its Iliac Hand card 1 in 5, 20 Marks, Deadlands Embers and Weir Keys, twice at a Valkynaz's. Feats, titles, auras, boards (the Descent, the Depths and the Deepest Redoubts) and the herald mark progress, a breach survives a server restart and can be returned to after a reload, and the Spire is rebuilt as the Fractured Battlespire with its own air, light, stone and score of fourteen songs. In the Athenaeum the Orrery's stones are turned to their true hours by looking just above a stone's gold ring, with the six riddles, each stone's name and hour and a "Stand closer to the stone." message on the HUD. The Spire's own words are in English for now. Bosses weigh each attack first and pick another, or move in, if it would leave someone nowhere to stand, and a boss that cannot fairly hit someone turns on the rest of the party for a few seconds. The bridges between sections now break apart as the Broken Way, crossed on Drifts, Beats, Crumbles, Pendulums, Vents and Rails, with forks as real choices, the rooms before the trial in fight halls and platforms checked across the whole jump; breaches opened before that update keep their old halls until they end. Every song is re-orchestrated with a driving fight score, halls that build to a peak and a ticking clock in a trial's last two minutes, and the Spire has its own ambience of the void's roar, war horns and breaking stone. Blows that hit a ward or a lord still asleep say "Warded" and blows that land nothing say "No footing", and none of them heal, leech or give set-bonus returns; a Court lord can be hit the moment it wakes, and Valkynaz Ilthys's banners can be broken with arrows and spells from the court floor. An Elite Breach can be opened past the Iliac Bay.
Enemy Sync ||| Enemy Sync keeps other players' enemies the same for everyone online. In busy areas enemies no longer die for one player and live on for another, or vanish when their controller leaves, dies or goes through a door, and at a crowded Starfall every player's enemies reach everyone in turn. Enemies run by a lagging player move smoothly for others, and on a slow computer enemies and the town watch keep pace with the player instead of moving at half speed. Online, all players in a game meet the same creatures, on the Bay and past it.
Enhanced Climbing ||| Enhanced Climbing is a feature switch under The world in Features, on by default; turning it off keeps Daggerfall's own climbing. Offline, the classic UI always uses classic climbing, and online it is on for everyone so every player can reach the same places. A city wall can be climbed: up the face, over the top onto the wall walk, across it and over the far side into a hang, where before the climb stopped under the top edge.
Falinesti ||| Falinesti is in the boughs of the Great Graht-Oak, with a walkway up the trunk and hide houses on its platforms and in its branches.
Fast Travel ||| Fast travel was fixed so that arriving in a raided town no longer crashes. It shows the journey's days again, which pass on the player's clock so the player arrives in the world's present, and cautious travel heals again. A place past the Bay cannot be fast travelled to; the player walks, sails or uses a portal.
Gathering ||| Gathering covers herb patches, ore veins, boulders, trees, bodies and fishing water. E acts on one only when it is looked at, in reach and unobstructed, and a node that cannot yet be worked says why on its row (tool, rank or daily limit). Actions use the loot list, picked by clicking, activating or tapping, with a small list window on the Classic skin and touch screens. The minigames are drawn on and around the crosshair with one short hint that fades, progress as diamonds and a sound for every strike, find, slip, splash and tug. Without a Materials Bag everything gathered goes in the pack even over its weight, and the message says so. A herb patch, vein, boulder or tree goes the moment the player finishes working it instead of waiting for the server to answer, coming back if the server refuses the harvest, and goods, XP and rank-ups arrive faster after each harvest. Gathering past the Veil works on the islands' herbs, trees, ore veins and boulders as in the Bay, each spot giving its full finds only once three players with accounts at least a week old have gathered there, and fishing in the Eltheric Ocean works. Every gathering act has its own sounds (felling, mining, herbs and the Basket, skinning, net fishing, goods going into the bag and a chime for a clean finish or a new rank), sounding the moment the tool hits and from where it happens, and on the Enhanced skin each axe or pick blow dips the view and hands slightly, following Technique camera motion. Other players see and hear the gathering, covered by their Attack sounds switch. The sounds are part of Enhanced sounds, and with it off or on the Classic skin Daggerfall's own sounds play, timed to the blows. The loot panel and its tooltip no longer cover skinning, or any other gathering minigame, while it plays. Each province past the Bay has many of its own resources to gather, and Cyrodiil has all of them.
Ghostfence ||| The Ghostfence is a ring of tall stone pylons 14 km from the center of Red Mountain, each holding a light with a wall of ghost light between them, visible from far away at night. It cannot be crossed except at Ghostgate on the south side, between two tall towers, and no farm, inn, temple or village is inside it and no road goes through it, though a route on the travel map goes through Ghostgate; trees, rocks and spires do not stand in the lava or its line.
Glyphs ||| Pressing a glyph on the account card hides it, and again shows it. Hiding only hides the icon, and anything the glyph lets the player do still works. On the enhanced skin the 2 and the Z are drawn so they can no longer be mistaken for each other. Origin is a $2 glyph, a dragon that bites its tail with a spark at its center and light moving on its scales.
Graht-Oaks ||| Giant graht-oaks, 134 m to 215 m tall, stand over the forests of Grahtwood, Malabal Tor and Greenshade, their crowns closing most of the sky from the forest floor. Under the leaves the light of the sun and moons is dimmer and greener, the air darker and the water shows the leaves, while above the crowns the light is full; the trunk and buttresses cannot be walked through.
Guilds ||| Guilds are player organisations founded on Renown 10 with restrictions on disbanding and standing reviews, functioning properly across regions, and online you can set entry permissions like an online home (Only me, My party, My guild, or Anyone). Townsfolk, shopkeepers, guild members, nobles and priests are dressed from a wardrobe of their own in Morrowind garments and colours, while a fighters' guild's people wear whatever armour came to hand. Online, a player is promoted the moment they qualify for the next rank by talking to a guild-service NPC at the hall, with no 28-day wait for a promotion, while demotion, probation and expulsion still wait 28 days and offline play is unchanged. Reputation earned before joining still counts, but a new member rises no faster than the guild records standing, about a rank and a half a day from the day they join, and the guild hall tells when the next promotion can come at the earliest. A member on probation is no longer expelled over reputation the guild still owes them, the probation being renewed instead. Online, Mages Guild quests "Guard the Guild" and "Protect an Honored Mage" need standing 10 and 20, and new apprentices are no longer handed them. Becoming a vampire no longer shuts the player out of the Dark Brotherhood or the Thieves Guild: membership and rank carry over when they turn and stay if they are cured, and a vampire who lost either gets it back, rank and all, the first time the game reads their guilds. A guild's quest-giver can always be asked for work as a stranger, even after an expulsion, each quest done raising standing with that guild by 5. The Thieves Guild and the Dark Brotherhood show new places outside the Iliac Bay, and the Mages Guild Scry page finds places outside it.
Harbourmaster ||| Every port's shipwright window has a Harbourmaster tab with the day's contracts while the ship lies at that port's quay: bounties on a named pirate, hunts in the crown's waters, cargo to carry to another port, a wreck's casks to raise and Admiralty surveys of ships through the spyglass. Contracts pay by level and rank and run to a deadline, finishing one raises standing with the crown while failing or giving one up lowers it, and three can be held at a time. A bounty's pirate waits in the waters named on the contract and the bounty is void with no loss of standing if someone else sinks her first. Cargo contracts send the player only to port towns on the sea, a harbourmaster's board stands only in a port town, and a cargo already held for an unreachable place is cancelled with a notice ("Contract void - ... No ship can make port there.") with standing with the crown unchanged. Each port past the Iliac Bay has a harbormaster who works for the nearest crown of the Bay, giving contracts, a letter of marque and the Admiralty carrack.
Helstrom ||| Helstrom stands in dark heart-mud, and its Hist is much larger, in a ring of tall stones.
Homes ||| Homes have a Who may enter setting, including My guild. Room furniture can be taken out, knightly order free houses are never owned by other players, graveyard crypts are not sold as homes online, and online homes/guild halls on the arena's block move to new houses on login without emptying storage. A phantom room tab in two-storey homes (often "Room 3") has been fixed. A home's chests no longer lose their contents when furniture fails to load, anything without a chest or box to go in being set down at the player's feet. When the servers are under heavy load the game waits longer between tries to load a town's homes, and loading a town's homes costs the servers half as many database requests. A home's furnishings load faster, especially when the server is busy. A home's crafting stations (forge, workbench, loom, mason's and jeweller's benches, alchemy and enchanting) can be used by the account's other characters as well as the buyer, while the storage still belongs to the character who bought it. A house can be bought in the Imperial City (not its castle) at the door with Buy it, priced by the size of the house and the city, and in the towns, villages and capitals outside the Bay, where a knightly order can also give one. Up to three houses can be owned in each region, online bought at their front doors, with the bank and Knightly Orders refusing only at three; selling at a bank shows each house in the town in turn, Yes selling the one shown, and the list for sale does not show a house the player owns.
Imperial City ||| The Imperial City stands on City Isle in the middle of Lake Rumare, 25 map pixels (about 20 km) wide, with a round White-Gold Tower 1,360 m tall rising from the Emperor's round palace, a 38 m wall with six watch towers and nine gates, Green Emperor Way and three ring roads. Its districts (Market, Arena District, Arboretum, Temple District, Talos Plaza, Elven Gardens, Waterfront and the Palace District) each have their own style and streets, the roofs can be crossed, and most buildings have named Daggerfall interiors that match their outside. Every door shows its name, the stairs are built stone, the Imperial Prison is on the north-east shore, and lamp-posts light the streets. Lake Rumare is part of the sea joined to the Niben through a canal, so a ship can sail up to the Waterfront, which is a port with passage to and from Senchal and Torval. Travel in its streets goes up to 60 times walking speed, and the districts are made ahead of the player on background workers.
Item Insurance ||| A magic or crafted item can be insured outside the open PvP zone with Insure on its card in the pack, for a fifth of its value, starting after two minutes. If a player kills the owner the killer cannot take an insured item, the insurance ends only when it saves the item, and a death never takes the cargo of the player's cart or the companions' packs.
Lands of Tamriel ||| The provinces past the Iliac Bay have places, the cities, towns and lairs of Arena, with coastal cities as ports and countryside and dungeons between them. The land follows a heightmap of Tamriel, with Red Mountain the highest ground, the next in Skyrim above 3,100 m and in the Jerall Mountains, Black Marsh low, and each region has its own small landforms. Coasts and province borders are smooth curves on the ground and on the maps, Cyrodiil has ten rivers and three lakes (Arrius, Poppad and Canulus), the seas round the rest of Tamriel have a real seabed, and high mountains have snow all year with their own snowline. Great trees stand at Falinesti and Mournhold, the strange canyons are gone, and saving outside the Bay loads the character and items at the correct height. Features > Lands of Tamriel turns it on or off and online it is always on.
Level-Up Window ||| The level-up window shows curse bonuses. It sits on the Growth tab's Level page, which shows attributes and, with a level up waiting, where to place points and press Ascend; leaving before pressing Ascend keeps the points unspent and the bonus never re-rolls, and the fanfare plays once per level. Online it shows progress to the next level in the skill points the skill sheet counts, such as 23/38 at mastered skills, where a point above 100 gives less to the level.
Leveling ||| Leveling is chosen at character creation on the "How will you grow?" screen, in the Enhanced Plus look. Online characters level the Daggerfall way, with Oblivion Remastered leveling shown locked as "Offline characters only", and online characters that were on Oblivion leveling switch to Daggerfall leveling the next time they are played, keeping their level, skills and any earned level-up. Offline the player can still pick either system. Online the highest level is 45, reached when five skills are mastered and a sixth is at 100; the sixth mastery makes that skill stronger but does not count toward the level. On joining, the game works out the level again from the skills, lowers a level that was more than the skills give and opens the level-up window for each level the character is owed, with the attribute points and health for each. A skill increases at the cost of the level the skills give. Offline characters do not change.
Mailbox ||| Each player with an account gets a letter from the DFO Team in the mailbox, beside the hourglass in the pause menu, with 10 Portal Stones that go into the online character's pack when claimed.
Main Quest ||| The main story no longer starts by itself: New characters in the Iliac Bay still wake in Privateer's Hold after the shipwreck, but the timer that made Lady Brisienna call the player a traitor after about two months does not run. The sealed commission of the Emperor, opened from the pack when the player is ready, sends the player to Daggerfall, where the story starts on arrival and the Emperor's agent sends for the player, with no time limit until then; a character who starts another way, such as a family heir, gets the commission in the pack. The commission is also a writ of passage for free sailing to Daggerfall, and the Quests page shows each step and the wait for Lady Brisienna's letter.
Maps ||| New setting: Settings → Interface → Maps → Map text and icons - Auto, or 0.75× to 2×. A map read outside the Bay, in the wilderness too, shows a new place.
Market ||| The Market is a Notice Board tab for trading in Marks or gold, featuring grouped views (Buy, Sell, History), silver display, column names, supplier counters, and detailed split lists. Listings stand 72 hours for a 1% fee with immediate sales (minus 5% tax) and courier delivery, with strict gold/Drakes currency separation and item exclusions. Seat boards, market sales, and courier deliveries no longer slow down account services in regions where a guild holds a Charter. Market stalls look the way the pack draws them, with the keeper behind the counter and goods on the counter. Players can buy and sell between each province and the Iliac Bay, couriers carrying the goods, and the market, writ boards, guild writs, contracts and the Watch work in the towns past the Bay.
Mentoring ||| A player is a mentor only when a party member at least 5 levels below, with the mentor's level at least 1.5 times theirs, is near them for 3 seconds, and Mentor Mode stops 30 seconds after that member goes away or at once if the mentor leaves the party; it changes only when a fight ends or after 30 seconds. The mentor fights 3 levels above the party member or a quarter above their level, whichever is higher, with enemies doing at most 2 times their usual damage. Firmament combat stars, specializations and skills above 100 work at a lower percentage, craft and trade stars at full strength, skills above 100 train when fighting with the party, the Rare and Legendary chance is not reduced, and the mentored member trains skills 20% faster up to 100. The HUD shows a Mentoring or Mentored icon, the Party tab shows each member's level, and chat gives one message when it starts and one when it stops.
Mournhold ||| Mournhold follows the plan of Tribunal: the High Fane to the north, the Royal Palace at the centre, Godsreach to the west, the Great Bazaar to the east, and to the south Plaza Brindisi Dorom, a garden around the statue of Almalexia in her fight with Mehrunes Dagon.
Named Beasts ||| From the hunter rank Stalker, each bounty board shows a named beast each week: an elite enemy with two boss traits, whose name party members also see. It gives the gold of three bounty groups and a Mounted Trophy, which can be put on a wall of the player's home. Bounty boards past the Iliac Bay post the week's named beast too.
Oblivion Gates ||| Oblivion Gates are nightly raid events featuring a handcrafted model of cracked basalt and fire with multi-court Warden boss fights, damage charts, and rewards, alongside tavern card games like Iliac Hand played against regulars or online players for stakes and collection cards. The Oblivion Gate boss's own mark and his host's paths no longer shade large areas of empty ground, so the ground markings in a boss AoE frame cost about a quarter less to draw. Gates open in all eight provinces past the Iliac Bay but not Cyrodiil, which is the open PvP zone, and closing one gives the player's kill influence to the seats of that province; the gate sites are found in small steps so the game does not stop.
Ocean Holes ||| Ocean holes open in the seas past the Iliac Bay and in the Eltheric Ocean, but online none opens in the open PvP zone of Cyrodiil, and a hall in the zone no longer shows the Ocean Holes tutorial.
Open PvP Zone ||| Cyrodiil is the open PvP zone, exactly the province with its lakes and rivers, and the red line, fog and edge of the zone map follow its border; the map shows the zone offline too, with its fog, red line and rings, and its city and town names. The streets of each city, town and village are a safe zone where no player can hit or cast at the player, and a message tells when the player enters or leaves; the open land, roads, dungeons, halls, farms, manors, inns and temples are not safe. A monster's kill returns the player to a town of Cyrodiil in the same ring and a player's kill to the nearest town outside the zone. For a minute after a hit between players items cannot be put into storage, a second kill by the same player within 30 minutes takes nothing, and for 60 seconds after entering or coming back to life no player can hurt the player, ended by attacking a player or taking items from remains. The Wrothgarian Mountains are a usual region again, and the Lake Rumare and Niben inside the province are part of the zone.
Overworld ||| The Overworld is a travel view over the real world, where the player clicks to travel and sees other players, weather from above, the sea, shared roaming bands, camps and dungeons. The controls are one compact block in the bottom-right corner, with a Path switch that is remembered: Roads (default) prefers road travel and Free walks across country around mountains and across water. A Show section filters Towns, Distant, Dungeons, Enemies, Travellers and Gathering groups, and since 0.1.6646 these filters sit in their own box at the bottom left, which folds away and can be moved like the rest of the HUD. A Nodes button picks how far gathering groups show, and right-clicking the map places a waypoint (see Waypoints). Double-clicking a band or camp marker asks "Attack the Zombie band?", and Yes travels straight toward them at full speed. Players close together show as one larger dot, only the six nearest show full title, Renown, tag and glyphs, towns with a carriage driver show a wheel, and friends' names are blue and guild-mates' violet, with player filters on both maps. Hovering a capturable town shows who holds it and this week's battle. Routes go around World of Daggerfall mountains, and rain, snow, sand and wind fill the air in front of the camera. The dungeons near the player (Daggerfall's own, random dungeons online and a standing Abyss Dungeon) show their own entrance model, enlarged so they can be seen from above, and the Overworld no longer refuses a walk over mountains. At high travel speeds (x100) the character, the ring under them and the route line move smoothly, the speed no longer drops to x60 for the rest of a trip after a small gap in the road, and the ring under the player is smaller, centred on their feet and rimmed dark. The route is a trail of gold dots fixed to the ground, the clicked spot is a gold X (a slightly larger one for a town), and map dots and diamonds are symbols in the same colours: a skull for roaming bands, a stone arch for dungeons (red if undiscovered, brass if found), a person for party members and other players (two for a crowd), a backpack for pilgrims and pedlars, and a pickaxe, pine, leaf, paw print and fish for Mining, Logging, Herbalism, Hunting and Fishing nodes. Ships, their map lookups and wagon harness ropes do the same work for less effort and memory garbage, so walking the coast runs faster. A found dungeon shows as a dungeon on the Overworld grid. Overworld travel no longer drops to 1x speed and stays there, and land that fails to load is loaded again instead of leaving a hole. Travelling fast no longer spends time building ground already passed, and the land ahead loads up to three times faster, so holes in the view and drops to 1x speed are much rarer, including on straight roads. While ground is still loading the view may run at about 30 fps instead of 60 until the land is built. Past the Iliac Bay the Overworld shows each city, town, village, farm, inn and temple by name (a dungeon must be found first), with a capital as one town, and a journey goes on the roads, telling the player at once if no way on land reaches a place; the far towns show at the edge of the screen, a quest mark starts a journey, walled cities show a carriage mark, and the dungeons near the player can be found, seen and travelled to.
Party Travel ||| Party travel lets the leader pick a destination while the party readies up, with members answering Ready or Stay behind from the Party tab, and the leader keeps the tab and a ready-up message that updates as people answer. A member following the leader's fast travel is not dropped when an enemy reaches them while waiting, followers wait for room beside the leader and for a leader whose journey is slow to load, and the leader's place is offered again after a journey is sent back. Party members aboard the player's ship cross the Veil with them and pay no fare. After a party journey that cannot finish, the player returns to the helm with sails up and the ship moving, a leader who goes to land the player's game does not show brings the player to the nearest place it shows, and a ship left behind stays at her own place on the map without being heard from far away.
Passage Under Sail ||| Friendly merchant and navy ships moored at a port's quays can carry the player: look at her hull and press Activate, and her captain lists every port the player knows, nearest first, with the direction it lies and the fare. After confirming, the player boards at her helm and the voyage is sailed across the Overworld, with nothing paid until she sets sail. If the voyage stops she sails on by herself, at the far port she lands the player, and stepping off her anywhere ends the passage, anything put in her hold coming back to the pack. She is never the player's own ship. Online, any player at the port can book a ship, other players can board and ride along, and once she has sailed she is gone from that berth for the rest of the day. "Under Sail" counts a voyage in any boat, counted from where the player last lay still, and running aground in the next harbour or leaving the helm as the ship comes in counts as making port. A voyage survives a reload or a crash between the two ports, Pencart's harbour can be reached, and the harbourmaster says that a port counts when it is another port's harbour a quarter league off or more. Ships sail from Daggerfall to each port past the Bay and back, round the provinces between, and a passage to a port outside the Bay continues after a save is loaded; ports found before stay on the ship captains' list. The commission of a province is also the Emperor's writ of passage, so a friendly ship's master sails the player to Daggerfall free of charge until arrival there.
Project Legacy ||| Project Legacy is built-in and enabled by default, where characters found houses with surnames and seats, choosing death mechanics (Enduring, Bloodline, No lineage). Features include inherited stats/reputation, bequests, realm synchronization, permadeath protections, vampire/werewolf/wereboar Enduring exemptions, a fourth Family tab page ("How it works") explaining rules/inheritance/heirlooms/marriage/symbols, tree links ("New to the family?"), and wrapping long names. Passing the mantle online can no longer overwrite a sibling with the new heir's name and class, heirs resumed from the Online page start at the family seat instead of Privateer's Hold, and a home owned on a farm or manor can be made the family seat, with family members met there beginning their story there. The remains of the house's fallen appear where they fell, as a body with a line of light over it in dungeons and in the open, pressed like any body to take back their bones, heirloom and share of the purse, and only in the dungeon the relative actually fell in. Whether the player is "in a fight" is judged where they are, so remains no longer stay shut because of an enemy back in the street, and a duel, the player's own arena bout or a hostile ship within reach counts as a fight, also for Play as, Pass the mantle and weddings. Putting the bones back no longer leaves the death quest stuck. The family's record is checked against the family's own rules when it is saved to the realm. An heir whose family seat is outside the Iliac Bay is born at that seat, and one of a family with no seat in the nearest town to where the family member died.
Quests ||| Quests feature fixes for shared progress, combat visibility/hits for nearby players, sky-based timing, quest-givers, town layout compatibility, abandoned/reclaimed quest systems without timer/item loss, and dungeon sizing placement fixes. Quest monsters appear only for the quest's owner and the owner's party, in dungeons, buildings and outdoors, for side quests and the main quest. Other players cannot see them, hit them or be attacked by them, party members holding the same quest still get kill credit, and a player standing next to the owner may see them fighting something they cannot see. Quest enemies, like the Fighters Guild's rats, appear in shops as well as houses, and quests whose shop is in another town, such as "Open a Chest", show that town in the quest list, on the travel map and on the compass. When a party member's progress on a shared quest reveals a place on their map it appears on the player's too (Settings > Repair active quests fixes a copy that already says "Not on your map yet"), and answering "No" by mistake in The Lost Child no longer locks the quest. A stuck quest can be restarted from its first step by pressing Restart twice in the pause window's Quests tab or in the chronicle (main-story steps included), picking fresh places and clearing its old items, links and topics. A party member's kill no longer steals the player's own copy of a main-story foe, quest markers in small and medium buildings sit inside the building, quest foes no longer stand on a hall's ceiling, the player is told when a quest's foe falls (HUD, chat and classic line) with the quest box ignoring presses for a moment, and an abandoned quest's item can be dropped and is handed back if the quest is reclaimed. Daedra summoning whose quest cannot be offered where the player is no longer takes their gold or marks the Daedra as summoned. Quests no longer pick another player's home as a quest residence, and if a quest's residence is in another player's home its door shows the quest's name, such as "The Hawkley Residence (Aldric's home)". A foe that appears inside a pillar is moved out beside it. A stuck quest can also be restarted from its first step by pressing Restart twice in the pause window's Quests tab or in the chronicle. Quest dungeons are always the full Daggerfall layout, so quest targets like the main quest's mummy can no longer end up unreachable from the entrance of a smaller online dungeon, and Quest Repair in a shared dungeon tells the player where the quest's things are instead of "Nothing was missing". Online, "Guard the Guild" needs Mages Guild standing 10 and "Protect an Honored Mage" standing 20. The Possessed Child can turn out to be a scam (the child fakes, the Daedroth never appears and the child runs off with the relic), and a journal entry now records what happened and sends the player back to the tavern where the guardian was staying. The world map shows a quest place past the Bay, such as a start dungeon, with its quest diamond, the compass points to it, and Find it on the map works for such places, though only with the Enhanced map. Quest givers past the Bay give work in other towns and dungeons of their province and in each district of the Imperial City, nobles in palaces give noble work, quest text gives directions and the correct province name for places outside the Bay, and people know those places and people. The Quests page shows the wait for Lady Brisienna's letter.
Raids ||| A town saved from raiders stays saved when the world's clock is corrected and pays the player once, and guards and townspeople are safe from the player's blade and horse during a raid. Raids are named 15 minutes before they start, including raids early in the game day, and show on the World Events board. Raids come in kinds: Assault (waves with a pause between, the last wave the party's leader with two boss traits), Siege of the Standard (keep the Standard-Bearer alive), Exodus (guide three refugees to the square) and the classic Raid. A town that is held gives more reputation, half again the gold and one extra Magic-or-better item. Raiders attack towns in each province outside the Iliac Bay except Cyrodiil, with each province's own raids each day made of the people and monsters living there; a message arrives when the player is in the same district, and the world map shows a town raiders attack.
Red Mountain ||| Red Mountain is a very large volcano at the center of Vvardenfell, its top about 7,300 m above the sea and visible on the horizon at more than 300 km on a clear day. Its crater is 5 km wide with a lake of lava, lava flows in three deep channels down from it, a large column of ash rises with the crater's fire lighting its underside, and snow never stays on it. It is part of Features > Landforms, always on online.
Reforge ||| The Reforge is a service of the Mages Guild. It always swaps a line for a different kind of line, on any line, as often as the player can afford, each reforge on the same piece costing twice the last, and Technique lines cannot be reforged though honing still raises them. Imbue raises a plain piece to Magic, or a found Magic piece to Rare, for Welkynd Shards and gold, with its lines rolled fresh. A reforge keeps a name the player gave the piece at the Item Maker. A Reforge on armor uses the same type frequencies as armor loot. The Imbue and the Reforge give each line at random, with no preference for the weapon's skill and damage no more often the first line; items dropped by monsters or crafted do not change.
Regional Creatures ||| The creatures past the Bay are those of the lore, by day and by night, with each province's towns facing their own dangers at night: wolves, bears, sabre cats and trolls in Skyrim; orcs, goblins, ogres and dragonlings in Wrothgar; giant scorpions in the Alik'r; imps, minotaurs, mountain lions, goblins and boars in Cyrodiil; Lizard Men in Black Marsh; scamps, daedroth and fire atronachs in the Ashlands and Red Mountain; and mudcrabs on the coasts. Water creatures stay in the water, a new character does not meet only strong monsters in the peaks, and no dragons appear. With Daggerfall Enemy Expansion its creatures appear, and without it a classic creature replaces one only where it fits.
Regional Flora ||| 33 regions in Skyrim, High Rock, Hammerfell, Summerset, Elsweyr, Black Marsh and Morrowind have their own ground colors, plants and landmarks, while Cyrodiil, Valenwood and the Blackwood area of Black Marsh do not change. The ground keeps the grain of the texture pack in the region's colors, each region has its own trees and amount of forest, and grass grows on each region's grassland but not on ash, snow, stones, bare rock or dunes. Examples are emperor parasol mushrooms in the Ascadian Isles, violet mushrooms and glowstalks in Deshaan, obelisks and xylaria spikes in the Ashlands, sandstone hoodoos in Elsweyr and the Alik'r, Hist trees and reeds in Black Marsh and vineyards on the Vine Coast; mushroom stems, spires, crags and trunks cannot be walked through, reeds and vineyard rows can.
Regions of Tamriel ||| Each province past the Bay has the regions its lore names, 44 in all, each with its own ground, plants, weather, countryside and creatures, and blending at the borders like the climates of the Bay: Skyrim (the Frozen Coast, the Tundra Plains, the Pinewoods, the Reach and the High Peaks), High Rock, Hammerfell, Sumurset Isle, Valenwood, Elsweyr, Black Marsh, Morrowind and Cyrodiil. Each city is in its own region. Cyrodiil is jungle with the Imperial City in the grassland of the Nibenay Valley, the Colovian uplands and Blackwood are jungle, the Gold Coast deciduous forest, the high ranges have snowy-peak regions, and Red Mountain is ash and fire with no snow.
Revenants ||| Revenants are special foes that kill the player or escape them and return to hunt. Elite and Champion foes of level 3 or more that kill the player become their revenant, as do special foes that flee below 20% health and anything that finishes the player with a spell or poison. They rank up each kill or escape to rank 5, each rank adding +25% health and +10% damage, and up to 5 can hunt at once. Each has a personality (Brutal, Witty, Humorous, Arrogant, Cold, Zealous, Unhinged, Honourable, Craven or Weary) and a named epithet, and learns from how it was beaten, returning with up to three adaptations, never immunity, and a hidden weakness that takes 50% more once revealed. From rank 3 its will must be broken before it kneels. A beaten revenant kneels and the player chooses Kill or Spare in the loot window: Kill is an execution that drops a unique Magic, Rare or Legendary weapon of its own plus all it carried, and Spare swears it as a companion for good, while leaving it 90 seconds makes it escape and rank up. Each has a lair, kin and a signature attack, and one that kills the player online takes an unlocked item. Under Project Legacy the revenant that killed an ancestor comes after their heir. Online, revenants wait for Renown 10: below it no foe becomes a revenant or breaks and runs to become one and none comes back, while existing revenants are held and marked Held on the Revenants page with when they will come. Revenants come back a little less often everywhere, a revenant that is due now taking 2 in 5 encounter chances instead of 1 in 2. Another player's Disintegrate no longer deletes the player's revenant outright but counts as a killing blow, and only the owner decides their revenant's fate. Not every elite or champion can become a revenant: about one in four is marked Unquiet ("Unquiet Elite Orc") and only a marked foe can rise, at most three hunt the player at once, and one comes back after two to four days. A marked foe breaks and runs at a third of its health one time in two, so most revenants are born by getting away, and a revenant ranks up every time it gets away (killing the player or tearing away from the killing blow ranks it only every second time, and from rank II its will must be broken or it tears away). When one gets away in the open it goes to ground in the nearest dungeon and leaves a trail, weakened at 60% of its health if reached within twelve hours, and left alone its wounds heal, the oldest every five days. From rank II each flees its own way (Smoke for people, Blink for casters, spirits, daedra and atronachs, Sink for skeletons, zombies, mummies, vampires and spriggans, Bolt for beasts and monsters), the blow that broke it leaves a wound (a blade bleeds it, a hammer or fist cracks it, an axe maims it, an arrow lames it, an element sears it, three kept), and it wants Vengeance, Mastery or Plunder by temperament, with omens on the day it returns. Each rank adds +40% health, +15% damage and +3 levels for a class foe (sworn revenants keep the old values), and a rank-V revenant that gets away once more becomes the Dread, which goes to ground for good, never comes to the player, has half again its health, makes two last stands and drops a Legendary trophy. The player's own Disintegrate no longer deletes a revenant: it still breaks through the last stand and will but brings the revenant to its knees like any killing blow, to Kill (its trophy weapon drops) or Spare (it joins as a companion). Revenants have lairs past the Iliac Bay, where a bounty board gives hunts in their dungeons and the map shows their circles.
Roads ||| A road crash (ROAD-CRASH) was fixed, and roads are painted onto new terrain about five times faster. Roads and tracks no longer climb every hill. Every gate of a walled city has a road, through its middle, and the road ringing a town goes all the way round with edges that blend into the ground. Roads and tracks run to the hamlets, villages, inns, temples, manors, shrines and covens past the Bay, with farms, homes and dungeons beside them; they follow the land around hills and lakes, along shores and through passes, cross rivers at narrow fords, curve at turns and never stop in the open or run into the sea. The roads of the Bay continue into the land past it at 14 places, Cyrodiil has eight named roads and the Red Ring Road circles the Imperial City, and the game lays the roads past the Bay in the background and keeps them, laying them again the first time this version is played.
Sea Chart ||| The chart past the Veil (opened with the map key) uses the game's own enhanced font, with names drawn after every island marker with a dark outline, each on whichever side of its island is clear. A depth ruler along the top names each stretch of water in view (the Shallows, the Reaches, the Deep Aether, the Abyss, the Veil and the world's edge), each depth band is its own shade of water, a league grid replaces the old stars and the world's edge is marked. Each island type has its own shape as well as colour (a port is round with a harbour dot, a ruin a diamond, a wild isle a peak, a bare rock a cross), larger islands show their real size when zoomed in, Veilhaven is ringed in gold, and a key under the chart explains every icon. A legend's haunt is a red-washed square while she sails and hatched grey while she lies sunk, the player's ship is a white arrow in a brass ring with an edge arrow giving the distance when off-screen, and pointing at or tapping an island rings it, draws a bearing line and shows a card with its name, type and distance. The chart fits the screen at any size and its words follow the chosen language, though the sea's own names are still in English for now. The ship position map shows the player's position outside the Bay on a map of all Tamriel.
Sea Serpent ||| Every four hours Sethrakul the Old Coil, a great serpent, rises on a packet lane in open sea. Harbour bells ring fifteen minutes before, chat names the port and the map rings its waters, and players sail out and fight it with their ships' guns, each ship that joins making it tougher. It lashes with its tail, rams down a lane, bursts up under its mark and spits venom, from two-thirds health the Coil wraps a ship (broken by focus fire to stun it), and from one-third the Maelstrom drags ships toward a whirlpool's eye. It no longer teleports but swims every move, with a wake showing where it is going, and aims where the player's ship will be when an attack lands, surging at the player on the surface when out of reach and giving up after about 12 seconds of being outsailed. While exactly two ships fight it its attacks deal two thirds of their damage, so two ships can win, though one alone cannot in fair weather and three or more take full damage. A broadside is judged where the serpent was when the shot landed. A storm closes its waters fifteen minutes after it rises and ten minutes before it dives, every ship whose guns did a tenth of her share earns a hoard of gold and Magic-or-better pieces including a Deadlands Ember, and each serpent slain is worth six quests of Renown and pays 40 silver (20 for a ship standing by), toward the same 150 silver a day. Sethrakul's waters stay open until it dives, so the 08:00 storm no longer locks ships out, and the serpent has a sixth less health, so two ships at average gunnery win every time in about 8.5 minutes though one ship alone still cannot. Inside its waters a gale fills the sails from every direction and ships turn 1.5 times faster, its blows throw a ship half as far and tear half as much canvas, it is 1.6 times thicker so slow cannonballs fired with lag still land, and players on someone else's ship can hurt it with their own bows and attack spells. Ships have a wide invisible buffer and bounce off each other, rewards no longer depend on being near when it dies, a real part of the damage or a quarter of the fight within range earns a hoard, and party members of any ship that dealt heavy damage also get one if they came to the fight. Killing the serpent has a rare chance to grant the title Coilbreaker (1 in 20 kills) and the aura The Maelstrom (1 in 50), a three-armed whirlpool of tide and foam around the player's feet, kept for good and announced in a popup the first time. A ship that did damage may also win The Sunken Sovereign, about one kill in a hundred. It can appear on each sea lane of Tamriel.
Seats ||| Seat towns feature palace charters, map markers, and kingdom banners, with chapters featuring officer and Master ranks determined by accumulated merit, reputation, and tenure to provide unique services and focus settings. The weekly Turning hands out Charters again, with only one request at a time settling the week so the servers are not swamped, and acting at a seat in the moment the week is turning says "The week is turning at the seats. Try again in a moment." and is never applied to a half-turned week. Town seats are worked out once a minute for the whole server instead of by every server process on every request, so the seats list, Watch claims, Levies, Tithes and sieges load faster and put far less strain on the database. From the first week of Season 1 a guild holds a full region, with one Charter and one siege at its chief town and the region's other towns as holdings.
Ships at Sea ||| Pirates, merchantmen and the navies of Daggerfall, Wayrest and Sentinel sail the Iliac Bay, each with a name and a captain and sailing the same boats the player can own. Pirates attack anything, merchantmen run, and a navy hunts pirates and the player once notorious enough, with most pirates only coming for a ship they outgun or one crippled and running from one that outguns them. Ships sail by the wind, keep off the rocks and fight broadside to broadside, a hostile ship nearby counts as an enemy nearby blocking fast travel and rest, and ships fight each other on their own, with the navy hearing cannon fire and sailing toward it. When a ship strikes to another the winner boards her and sets her on fire, a ship with no crew left surrenders, a galley's ram forces surrender rather than sinking, and packets sail lanes between neighbouring port towns on one shared schedule, berthing, waiting and sailing on. Friendly ships have green health bars and turn hostile if fired on, ships are named for their trade and waters, and the Ships at sea setting decides how many are out, none when it is off. Warm Ashes' Overworld raiders are real ships at sea. Sea ships fly their own colours: navies use their kingdom's paint and royal ensign (Daggerfall's blue with the dagger, Wayrest's crimson with the rose, Sentinel's gold with the sun), merchant ships belong to one of eight trading houses with the house's paint and mark (the Isles' teal past the Veil), pirates use one of six crews' dark paint and black flags with false colours painted as the merchant house until the black flag is raised, and legendary ships have their own colours. A ship that surrenders, sinks or is captured lowers its flag, and cloth flags show on ships within 450 m (up to 24 at once) with the old pennant further away. Packet ships sail between the ports of the Bay and those past it, and a boat plans its route past the Bay much faster.
Shipyards ||| Port towns have a shipyard on their seaward edge, its back to the water, where the shipmaster sells every ship Come Sail Away builds, from the Rowboat to the Carrack and the Large Galley, and buys the player's ships back. One window holds Ships for sale (each hull's size, crew, guns and price, with buying asking for confirmation), Your fleet (where each ship lies, her worth and what the shipmaster will pay, with Bring her here, lay up, rename and sell, a big sale paid as a letter of credit) and the Shipwright and Refits, which need Naval Combat on. The Rowboat goes into the pack as parts and every other ship's deed goes into the Fleet laid up at that port. The shipmaster will not buy a ship with cargo in her hold, anything in her cabin or her crew ashore with the player, or one that came into the realm through customs, and a sold ship's cabin furniture is sold too with half its value going to the bank. Townsfolk tell the player where the shipyard is and it is marked on the town map and as an anchor on the Overworld. Shipmaster cards show each ship's own 3D picture, and the Reforge, shipyard and naval-yard pages and a merchant's options each have their own icon. Towns away from the open sea, such as Ilessan Hills, no longer have a shipyard and a lighthouse beside a pond. The shipyard's fleet page reads properly again. Solitude, Alinor, Torval and the Imperial City's Waterfront have a shipyard, a crew hall, a lighthouse and docks, and their banks sell ships.
Siege Camp ||| At the Turning, a winning camp's Ram Kits go to the battle if the seat has a Gatehouse. Members can give fortification materials from their Stores or bag to the stockpile or Siege Camp of the guild at a Notice Board of the region.
Sky-Ketch ||| The Mzunthar is a Rourken sky-ketch that crosses the Iliac Bay every ten hours, online only, sighted an hour before she arrives and keeping station over the fields east of Daggerfall for seven hours. Each party of up to eight boards its own ship for a raid through the Boarding Watch (five waves), the Gallery of Hammers, the Starved Engine, the Sailwalk and the Tonal Warden, all tougher since 0.1.11269. A questline from Imrah Bel-Tavir of Sentinel and two books go with her, and her hold is the only source of the Dwarven Thunderlock and Pistolet. A player who dies with a companion still standing lies where they fell for 60 seconds, awaiting a party member's Resurrect. Its messages, tracker, map card, questline text, books and Discord announcements say "it" and use plainer words. Boarding was repaired and a fall from it wakes the player in the fields at a quarter of their health; crews aboard when it weighs anchor have 45 minutes to finish, and a Tonal Warden fight is lost only after the bridge has been empty for two minutes. A Ketch comes to the crown capital of each province (Daggerfall, Sentinel, Solitude, Mournhold, Alinor, Falinesti, Torval and Helstrom), each on the same ten-hour cycle with voyages 1 hour 15 minutes apart, and the Timers window and map show the province's Ketch with Daggerfall's.
Snow ||| With Snowless Swamps and Jungles switched on, swamps and jungles get no deep snow or falling snow in winter, and rain/snow no longer become choppy after hours of open play. In Skyrim and all cold land outside the Iliac Bay the snow is deep in winter, leaves tracks and gives roads snow banks; with Landforms on, high mountains keep snow all year, each region has its own snowline, which comes lower in winter, and steep faces near the snow show bare rock.
Solitude ||| Solitude stands high on a headland above the Sea of Ghosts, with cliffs falling to the water on the west, north and east. It has two levels: the lower Well District inside the main gate, and an upper city 6 metres higher, held up by a stone wall with three stairs, that holds the Avenues, the Bards College, the Hall of the Dead, the Temple and Castle Dour. The great arch crosses the mouth of the Karth 42 metres above the water from the Blue Palace, which stands on its tall sea stack, to a terrace above the west wall with a stair down to the upper city. The docks, shipyard and lighthouse are at the water below the north cliff, reached by the Docks Way down from the north gate, and the Gate Road zigzags up the slope to the main gate; a stair goes up from the lane of the main gate to the north court of Castle Dour, and a ramp inside its west gate.
Spawned Dungeons ||| Spawned dungeons are ported and expire, 2 in-game days when cleared and 7 in general. They are announced on their own pixel in metres, appear only where the ground is dry land above the beach line and no longer on beaches or at sea, and pixels that once held a shoreline spawn are now empty land or sea for everyone. Random dungeons in the online wilderness are a quarter fewer; one that remains is the same place as before. Everyone stands on the same ground around one, whether the dungeon is still around or has expired, and an expired one leaves its level clearing and ground tiles until it comes back. Online, spawned dungeons are in all of Tamriel, some of them Elite as in the Bay, and the holes in the ground past the Bay where a spawned dungeon stood on the land were repaired.
Staff Commands ||| Staff chat commands for Developer, Dungeon Master and Shadow Fang (STAFF1) were added in 0.1.4644. A staff unfreeze covers only the problem the staff looked at, and a frozen character's notices say what is frozen and, for a staff freeze, how to contact the staff. /town audit shows the capitals of a province first, 400 towns a page, and /town audit 2 the next page.
Staff Events ||| Staff events are a staff tool for running a monster event on a city or area, with a hidden kill meter and a yellow mini boss. The mini boss always spawns and a boss appears each time the meter fills, every player sees the mini boss's real health, and it stands at the biggest group. Packs keep coming while the boss waits, more and faster with more players, and the reward is earned by kills alone. Staff can put a town event in a town outside the Iliac Bay and an area event past it, the list of places showing the provinces.
Staff Teleport ||| Staff teleport takes the player to the right interior in towns with the new layouts, and teleporting to a boat or cabin keeps them on the right boat and in their own cabin. Private rooms and homes still check ownership. /tp works to a player past the Iliac Bay, and a POI can stand past the Bay.
Standing ||| Standing is how regions and the law see the player. A negative standing now recovers faster, a point every 7 days instead of every 112, with the law or a guild. A screen shows every region that knows the player's name, how it sees them and what it will cost, every change from a crime, sentence, penance, contract or raid defence is announced in a line, and the Enhanced Plus Standing page has a Dress line showing what the outfit is doing. For a seat, a low Tithe (half the cap or less) gives +2 each week, a high one (over three quarters) gives minus 3, and a seat held without a challenge gives +5, with the seat's board saying which way the Standing moved since the last Turning.
